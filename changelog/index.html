newest first.

Brut is a Windows app in private beta. Approved seats get every release by email and download it from the same page they signed in on.

To update: sign in at getbrut.app/download and run the new installer over the old one. It upgrades in place; your projects, outputs and keys stay where they are. Between the versions listed here, smaller builds went to individual testers; they are folded into the release that followed.
1.1.52026-10-08

The models: Seedream, FLUX 3, Nano Banana 2.1, Seedance Fast and Mini, Haiku 5.5; Conform extends a picture; layers out of a render; the original back with only the edits

Everything since the 1.1.4 send. Five new models on every seat, three new moves on a picture, the proof sheet judged during the run, clip prompts written for the video model, a generated keyframe on any Storyboard card, Veo's rules on the node, and the fixes of six days of field runs.

  • Seedream 5.0 on your BytePlus key. Pro, Flash and Lite in every image menu, on the key you already hold for Seedance: $0.018 a Flash image, $0.045 on Pro ($0.09 at 2K), Lite at $0.035 up to 4K; up to 10 references (14 on Lite), masks as guidance, watermark off. Sizes are always explicit, and every Seedream edit node offers Same as the picture (the default on Annotate): the result comes back at the input's own pixels instead of a resampled tier. The rule that matters for films: Seedance accepts a Seedream text-to-image picture with a face in it (a one-picture character sheet included) and the frames of its own clips; every Seedream edit, every crop and every other maker's portrait is refused there. Image → Video on Seedance shows a green line naming the wired pictures that will pass and an amber one naming those that will not; a refusal names the picture and the rule. The Character Lab's note says the same: wire its design alone.
  • Decompose into layers. Right-click any picture → Decompose into layers… (or Decompose result… under a Layers node). One Seedream call (Flash or Pro, size led by Same as the picture, an optional "what to separate") returns a base and up to 16 RGBA layers with names, and Brut lands them as a new Sketchpad beside the source: base at the bottom, the layers in stacking order at their boxes, the original hidden on top for a one-click compare, the names and descriptions on the rows. Export PSD works at once; Find in ledger on the new Sketchpad reaches the paid row. Flash bills $0.018 per layer, base included; the dialog states the ceiling (17 outputs) and what four layers would cost before you pay; every way out spends nothing.
  • Annotate & Correct on Seedream. Pick Seedream Pro or Flash on the node and your marks travel as coordinates inside the prompt (a point for an arrow's tip, a box for a lasso): the clean picture goes out with its references, nothing drawn on it, nothing for the model to leave behind, each edit at its mark. The face says how the marks travel per model; a moved mark is an honest cache miss, a re-save with the same marks a hit. Seedream Lite reads no coordinates and stays off this node's menu.
  • Reconcile with input, rebuilt. A generative edit redraws the whole frame: a few pixels of drift everywhere, regenerated texture, a shifted tone. 🗇 Reconcile with input on an Annotate result now spawns the Layers node with the original as the base, lays the result back over it (the engine measures one global move, a shift and a stretch, from tiles that agree; under a pixel on Seedream, a few pixels on GPT Image 2.5, none on Nano Banana 2), masks what changed near your marks and composites at once: the original's exact pixels everywhere, the model's only where you asked. The Annotate node's own output is never touched; the Layers editor opens on an ordinary mask you can repaint. Model-agnostic: two pictures in.
  • FLUX 3 Image and FLUX Outpaint. Black Forest Labs joins the providers on one key (dashboard.bfl.ai, prepaid credits; ⚙ Settings shows the balance). FLUX 3 Image is in every image menu: 768 px to 4K, up to ten reference pictures, web grounding on by default, from $0.041 an image ($0.10 at 2K), also through OpenRouter. FLUX Outpaint is a painter inside Conform (below), the one that holds the known pixels: $0.10 per megapixel painted in high mode, a fast mode at about a sixth, a size menu (up to 4, 2 or 1 MP), the price shown before the paint.
  • Conform extends a picture. The Method menu gains extend beside crop (pad stays, free): the ✂ editor places the frame outside the picture (Shift locks the direction, the edges and the center snap), shows the painter bar (provider, model, size led by Same as the picture, GPT quality, the price for this still) and a note per still; ✦ Paint this still paints from the editor, ✦ Paint N stills from the face, ↻ per tile re-paints, 🕘 and 📌 per still keep and pin takes. With FLUX Outpaint the picture is held and only the band is painted, in multiples of 16 and under 4 MP (a larger frame is painted smaller, never scaled up). With an image model the band is painted from a prompt written per still by a vision model (what sits where, what continues past each edge) and the frame is redrawn; the readout says so. FLUX Outpaint is the default painter on a seat with a Black Forest Labs key, GPT Image 2.5 Flare elsewhere. The node's height follows its tiles.
  • Nano Banana 2.1, and Nano Banana 2 retires. Google's new Flash-tier image model is in every image menu at half Nano Banana 2's price: $0.034 at 1K, $0.05 at 2K, $0.076 at 4K, up to 14 references, thinking on, clean lettering, on your Gemini key or through OpenRouter. Google shuts Nano Banana 2 down on October 29, 2026: Annotate & Correct and the Correction Loop (now v2) start on 2.1, the model's own description on the node says it is retiring, and any node still on Nano Banana 2 needs a new pick before then.
  • Seedance 2.0 Fast and Mini, and the last frame. The two cheap Seedance 2.0 tiers in the video menus: 480p and 720p only, 4 to 15 seconds, first and last frame, nine references, audio; a 4-second 480p take on Mini is about $0.14, on Fast about $0.23 (list rates, native or through OpenRouter). Every Seedance clip now hands back its last frame: ⏭ Last frame → Image In on Image → Video drops it beside the node, ready to start the next shot; it also rides the ledger row. Seedance 2.0's 1080p and 4K stay on the full model.

Anthropic's new small model, in every text picker a day after release: $0.10 in, $0.50 out per million tokens, a 1M context, the reasoning dial (low to max) and the thinking switch (adaptive by default, a plain off). On Stills → Film (and any card holding a Director), the plan's run now renders the contact sheet at once and a review opens over the canvas: Take this sheet (a run whose sheet replays under it goes on without asking), ↻ New take, ✎ Reinvent N after ✗ on frames, or Stop (nothing downstream runs on an unjudged plan). Stills → Film ships as v3: GPT 5.6 Terra plans, the sheet and the keyframes on GPT Image 2.5 Flare at xhigh, clips on Kling 3.0 with audio, the knobs reduced to the Director's dials, the film aspect and one Quality; a 📖 Plan button and a headline on the card. Film Shots adapts every sent clip's prompt to the video model you picked (Kling, Seedance, Veo, Omni), so each model gets the kind of prompt it follows best. The table shows the rewrite with a ✦, the Director's text stays on hover, a plan edit rewrites that clip, a model switch to another family rewrites them all, the same family replays. A failed rewrite is loud and sends the Director's text. No knob: it is how Film Shots sends.

An invented shot you grounded on a still by hand comes back to its keyframe; a grounded shot or a shot you added yourself gets a generated keyframe of its own, its prompt and anchor stills written on the card. Every native Veo clip carries audio, and Google lists its rates with audio: Veo 3.1 $0.40 a second at 720p and 1080p, $0.60 at 4k; Fast $0.10 / $0.12 / $0.30; Lite $0.05 / $0.08.   • Fixes from the field: Marcel re-encodes a picture under 2048 px but over 5 MB before it goes to Claude (Anthropic's cap), so a large PNG no longer fails the turn; the Refine and Multi-Shot planner selects name their real default (Gemini 3.8 Flash); in the Annotate editor, once the marks list grew taller than the picture, the drawing overlay stretched with it and marks were shown and recorded lower than drawn (the request too): the frame now wraps the picture; a slow render past the client's wait says so in words ("the provider did not answer in time", re-roll) instead of a bare ReadTimeout, and the image clients wait longer for a 2K high-quality render; the Reconcile Layers node is wired original-first; Same as the picture keeps a Seedream edit at the input's exact size on Pro and Flash. The Workflows: fifteen ready-made cards, a gallery, Save project as, and Marcel sees what you name

Everything since the 1.1.3 send. The library now ships a bank of workflows, each one closed card with knobs on its face and a real before and after, and the library itself became a gallery. Around it: a project move that takes everything along, readable file names, Seedance draft takes, Marcel with eyes on the nodes you name, four new brains, and a long round of fixes from the field.

  • Fifteen workflows ship with Brut. Clay → Photoreal, Clay to Client Set, Time of Day (dawn, day, dusk, blue hour, night), Weather & Sky, Populate, Populate → Clip, Scene Dressing, Editorial Sheet, Look Transfer, Hero Still → Clip, Finish in 4K, Stills → Film, Reel Script, Character Lab and the Correction Loop, on four shelves: Architecture, Characters, Cinema, Text. Each drops as one closed card with knobs: a brief, a mode, the model of a stage. The image stages are pinned (Nano Banana Pro at 2K, GPT Image 2 for people); video and upscale stages ship unset, so the provider and its price stay yours, and the card says when a stage still needs a pick. Every card carries a real before → after cut from the workflow's own run on its own scene; Try with sample drops a card already fed with a shipped picture and shows the shipped result until you run it; Next chips under a result drop the workflow that usually follows, wired. A dropped card is frozen at its version and shows a vN in the library chip when a newer one exists; a closed card shows the key one of its stages is missing. Clip cards rest on the still they take and play their clip on hover. Marcel knows the bank: ask for a dusk version of a render and he drops Time of Day with the knob set.
  • Knobs on a composite card. Any closed composite can expose inner fields on its face: a prompt variant (the menu word is the label, the whole prompt the value), a text, a number, a tick list, or the model of a stage, route-aware. Flip a knob and only that stage re-renders. The clay cards carry a structured brief (Materials, Vegetation, Time of day, Atmosphere, Anything else; an empty line is the model's call) plus Size and Quality. The clip stage takes Duration, Resolution, Ratio and Audio from the picked video model's own menu, greyed in place where it has none; Prompt style greys the refiners written for another model; Move (eight camera moves), Speed and Steady camera complete it. ⚙ Add knob on any card of yours; Marcel reads and sets knobs by their label.
  • The library is a gallery. The book icon on the rail opens a full surface on the home's mold: From Brut and Yours as paged shelves, search, category chips (Architecture, Characters, Cinema, Text, plus the ones you name), presets grouped by family. Hover a card: the input, then the result. Every card says Add to canvas; a template lands as a group of live nodes named after it. Your own saves keep a before and an after when an image is wired in, and the save dialogs ask for a category from a pick list. The home's Start-from row is now one Workflows row: templates and composites together, the library one tile away.
  • Stills → Film, Reel Script and Finish in 4K are cards. The whole film chain as one composite with the Director's dials, the film aspect and the three models as knobs; Finish in 4K is its Next chip (an upscale stage inside a card would fail every run until set). A closed card's inlets now feed everything inside it: the Stills → Film card generates its keyframes and its proof sheet closed, and the keyframe confirm says when no proof sheet exists to follow yet.
  • Save project as. Work begun on the default project moves into a project of its own: the canvas, every output it references, the past takes behind the clock, the ledger rows and the named graph file. Shared files stay as copies; nothing is re-rendered, every cache stays warm. The first launch now asks for a project before anything else, so new work never lands on the default one. The same mechanism keeps a .brutpack or a NAS share warm on another machine: unchanged nodes replay free. (On 1.1.3 the move fell back to carrying the canvas alone; that was the 1.1.3 engine, this build carries the real thing.)
  • Readable output file names. Every output is named project_graph_node_provider_MODEL_date_job instead of a bare id, uploads are named after their graph, and a name shortens by itself near Windows' path limit instead of failing. Right-click any picture → Find in ledger turns it back into the run that made it (model, node, prompt, cost) and jumps to the node's history; File ▾ → Output file names… sets a pattern of your own per project. Old files keep their names.
  • Seedance draft takes. On Seedance 2.5, tick Draft take for a 480p clip billed like any 480p clip, then ⬆ Render final re-renders the same take at 1080p from the provider's own draft: same seed, same frames, only the pixels change, within seven days. On Image → Video and on Film Shots (a final tick per row, then Render N finals). The node, the Clip Board and the Sequence follow the final once it exists.

Write a node's id in a message and its current pictures go along: a Storyboard's card frames, a Director's proof sheet, a board's pool; the composer says so before you send, and a legend tells him which image is which. Right-click any picture → Send to chat; the 📎 picker is newest first and holds still; From disk…, paste and drop bring outside images in; up to 50 stills per message on every brain. His replies render bold, lists and quote blocks, so a prompt copied out of one pastes clean, and he never claims to have seen an image he was not sent. The chat stays on the latest line (a scroll parks it, your own message or run brings it back), Ctrl+C copies selected text even with nodes selected, and the right-click on text or a field offers Copy, Cut, Paste and Select all.
  • Four new brains, two corrected prices. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is Marcel's default brain (same price as Sonnet 5) and Claude Opus 5.5 joins the pickers (cheaper than Opus 5, thinking always on); GPT-6 Sol and Luna join on the OpenAI key or through OpenRouter, Luna the cheapest text model in Brut, and Marcel on a GPT brain acts from the first message (his tools run at reasoning none, the one setting OpenAI's chat API accepts with tools). Image → Video gained follow the input in the aspect menu (the start frame's real ratio, no bands on an odd still) and a Steady camera switch; Compose and multi-image edits on Gemini keep the scene's aspect on auto. 1.1.32026-09-21

A home screen, a History board, and the Sketchpad generates into a selection

Brut now opens on where you left off. Plus a board that shows every take a node ever made, the Layers editor round you asked for, snap to grid, and a round of fixes from the first beta reports.

  • The home screen. Brut opens on a home, not a bare canvas: a Continue card with your live graph and its latest results as the cover (hover it to flip through the last five), a What's new panel beside it, then every recent graph across all your projects in a paged gallery, each card badged with its project, its cover cycling through the graph's last results on hover, with a search box and a project filter. Start from lists the templates by type (Images, Video, Text) with the starters on their way marked soon; a Community workflows row is there, marked soon. The house icon on the left rail and File then Home bring it back any time; Esc or Continue returns to the canvas; New graph asks for a name first, so you never land on unsaved work; a checkbox at the bottom turns the launch landing off.
  • The History board. A board node that shows every past take of the generation nodes wired into it, newest first, one group per node with its model, date and cost, from the project ledger. Videos play in place, a pin on a tile lands on the source node, a tile drags out as an Image In. The answer to "I get lost in my generations".
  • Sketchpad: generate into a selection. Draw a marquee or the new ellipse (Shift+M cycles them; a late Shift makes a square or a circle, a late Alt grows from the center), press the spark, type what belongs there: the model fills that area of the flattened scene and the selection becomes the layer's mask. No marks needed.
  • The Layers editor round. Per-layer opacity, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y inside the editor, fills that follow the swatch (Alt+Backspace foreground, Ctrl+Backspace background), Ctrl+I to invert a mask, Shift-click straight strokes, the stamped-dab brush from the Sketchpad (no more beading along a curve), and the node composites the moment you press Use this stack, so it never sits un-run.
  • Snap to grid, and dots you can see. A magnet on the rail snaps dragged nodes to the canvas dots (a device setting, off by default); the dots themselves are bigger, on a 24-unit pitch, tuned separately for the dark and light themes.
  • You will know when a new build is out. At launch, while signed in, Brut compares your version with the latest installer and shows one line under the toolbar with a Download button when you are behind. Nothing new leaves the machine: the answer rides the existing sign-in heartbeat.
  • Multi-Shot's broken shot lists are fixed. A planner reply that comes back broken is now rare, and the shot planner retries once before giving up; the cinema planners got the same.
  • Fixes from the field: typing mid-text in the Prompt node, the Text Board tiles and the Storyboard card prompts no longer jumps the caret to the end (a 1.1.2 regression, every text field is now under test); a Delete or Backspace inside the Sketchpad or Layers editor can no longer remove the node behind it, and reopening the painter while its images were still loading no longer shows a single layer; Marcel's native Gemini brain acts again (tool calls carry their thought signature back) and Gemini's new refusal spelling gets its plain explanation; the Image to Video aspect select shows again on Seedance 2.5 text-to-video runs; the File menu lost its stray emoji and every item sits on one line; the blank taskbar icon from the early 1.1.2 installers is healed on install.
1.1.22026-09-12

The Sketchpad: draw over your images, then let the model finish the drawing

The first release after the Budapest conference ships the item you asked for most: a place to compose before you generate. Plus a real right-click menu on every image, wires you can route and color, and OpenAI's new image models.

  • The Sketchpad node. A painter inside Brut: brush and eraser with pen pressure, six brush presets, a Photoshop-grammar color panel (foreground and background, HSB field, recent colors), fill bucket, marquee and lasso selections, Shift-click straight strokes, Alt-click eyedropper. Wire your renders in and place them as layers: move, scale, rotate with on-canvas handles, set opacity, paint a mask on any layer, group layers into folders. Undo and redo inside the editor. The flattened image flows out of the node like any other image, and running it costs nothing.
  • Generation layers. Paint a few marks where you want something, press ✦, and one paid render lands as a new layer masked to your marks: the scene below stays yours, the model draws what you sketched, and you keep adjusting the layer's mask, opacity and position afterwards. A follow dial (loose, balanced, close) sets how literally the marks are read; a scope switch renders only the marked region on very large documents; every take stays in the layer's history and can be pinned back. Cost is shown before every render.
  • PSD export. Any Sketchpad document exports as a Photoshop file with every layer, mask, folder, name, opacity and color label intact, from the editor footer or the image's right-click menu.
  • The media right-click menu. Right-click any image or video in Brut, outputs and imports alike: Reveal in Explorer, Copy file path, Copy image (a video gives its poster frame), Export JPG (full resolution) and Export web JPG (2048 px), saved into the project's exports folder and revealed at once.
  • Wires you can shape. Three wire styles per graph (curved, straight, stepped) from the left rail, saved with the graph. Alt-click a wire to drop a reroute dot and route it around things; delete the dot and the wire heals. A wire menu: add reroute, insert node, color, delete.
  • Node color and wire tint. Right-click a node: Color, Run, Duplicate, Group, Collapse, Delete. Pick one of six hues and the node and every wire leaving it take it, through reroutes, on every selected node at once. Wires can carry their own color too. Dense graphs finally read at a glance.
  • GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst. OpenAI's new pair, measured live on forty runs: 2.5 at max quality bills exactly what GPT Image 2 bills at high, and 2.5 at high bills about GPT Image 2's medium, so the same picture costs less or a better one costs the same. Sizing is now always explicit (OpenAI's automatic size used to let the model shrink a 2K inpaint to 1254 px).
  • New planners. Gemini 3.8 Flash (Google's strongest Flash, same introductory price as 3.7) and GPT-6 Astra, available but not recommended for Brut's workloads: powerful and expensive, and it acts for Marcel only through OpenRouter for now. Also corrected: Gemini 3.7 Flash estimates had used Google's batch rate, half the real price.
  • One OpenRouter key reaches more. New routes for Nano Banana 2 Lite, Seedance 2.5 and Kling O1, so an OpenRouter-only seat runs the starter graph out of the box.
  • Sequence keeps your sound and your format. Assembled films keep each clip's audio (silent clips get silence, so mixed pools stay in sync, crossfades blend the sound) and adopt the first clip's aspect: vertical reels no longer ship pillarboxed.
  • Run reveals first, then asks. The toolbar's Run no longer generates blindly. Pass one replays every cached, pinned and free step for $0 and marks the nodes whose inputs changed with a needs a run badge (their last result stays on the face); if anything would bill, one dialog itemizes it with the estimate: Generate, or keep the cached state. Every generation node also shows its last result the moment a graph opens, so a reopened graph never looks empty.
  • Sketchpad, from the first exe round: the brush size/softness gesture works with a pen (no pointer lock, the Windows press-and-hold ring is harmless), the brush circle previews at the canvas center while a slider is dragged, a readout next to the pressure boxes shows what the last stroke sent (pen · 78 %, or mouse · no pressure when the tablet driver sends mouse events), and images decode fully before the first preview.
  • Polish: the inpaint mask editor got the Layers-editor feel (wheel zoom at the cursor, middle-drag pan, a brush cursor, [ and ] to size); Ctrl+C on a node with an image on the clipboard pastes the node again; the per-kind frame colors were dropped for a calmer canvas.
1.1.12026-08-31

The Director: the whole cinema crew in one call

In hard field testing on a real competition project, the four-agent chain lost a head-to-head against a single well-briefed model, so the planning side of the cinema pipeline was rebuilt around the winner; everything downstream is untouched.

  • The Director node. One agent, one call: it analyzes your stills and writes the concept, the look and the executable shot plan, with a real cinematic arc and no two shots alike. Brief and Style menus, People and Audio dials, shots and runtime. Your own brief and style arrive on labeled wires (saveable as Prompt presets): a wired brief composes with the menu and wins on conflict. Every clip carries a one-line ambient sound note for video models that produce audio.
