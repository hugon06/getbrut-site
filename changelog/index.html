What shipped,
newest first.

Brut is a Windows app in private beta. Approved seats get every release by email and download it from the same page they signed in on.

To update: sign in at getbrut.app/download and run the new installer over the old one. It upgrades in place; your projects, outputs and keys stay where they are. Between the versions listed here, smaller builds went to individual testers; they are folded into the release that followed.
1.1.22026-09-12

The Sketchpad: draw over your images, then let the model finish the drawing

The first release after the Budapest conference ships the item you asked for most: a place to compose before you generate. Plus a real right-click menu on every image, wires you can route and color, and OpenAI's new image models.

  • The Sketchpad node. A painter inside Brut: brush and eraser with pen pressure, six brush presets, a Photoshop-grammar color panel (foreground and background, HSB field, recent colors), fill bucket, marquee and lasso selections, Shift-click straight strokes, Alt-click eyedropper. Wire your renders in and place them as layers: move, scale, rotate with on-canvas handles, set opacity, paint a mask on any layer, group layers into folders. Undo and redo inside the editor. The flattened image flows out of the node like any other image, and running it costs nothing.
  • Generation layers. Paint a few marks where you want something, press ✦, and one paid render lands as a new layer masked to your marks: the scene below stays yours, the model draws what you sketched, and you keep adjusting the layer's mask, opacity and position afterwards. A follow dial (loose, balanced, close) sets how literally the marks are read; a scope switch renders only the marked region on very large documents; every take stays in the layer's history and can be pinned back. Cost is shown before every render.
  • PSD export. Any Sketchpad document exports as a Photoshop file with every layer, mask, folder, name, opacity and color label intact, from the editor footer or the image's right-click menu.
  • The media right-click menu. Right-click any image or video in Brut, outputs and imports alike: Reveal in Explorer, Copy file path, Copy image (a video gives its poster frame), Export JPG (full resolution) and Export web JPG (2048 px), saved into the project's exports folder and revealed at once.
  • Wires you can shape. Three wire styles per graph (curved, straight, stepped) from the left rail, saved with the graph. Alt-click a wire to drop a reroute dot and route it around things; delete the dot and the wire heals. A wire menu: add reroute, insert node, color, delete.
  • Node color and wire tint. Right-click a node: Color, Run, Duplicate, Group, Collapse, Delete. Pick one of six hues and the node and every wire leaving it take it, through reroutes, on every selected node at once. Wires can carry their own color too. Dense graphs finally read at a glance.
  • GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst. OpenAI's new pair, measured live on forty runs: 2.5 at max quality bills exactly what GPT Image 2 bills at high, and 2.5 at high bills about GPT Image 2's medium, so the same picture costs less or a better one costs the same. Sizing is now always explicit (OpenAI's automatic size used to let the model shrink a 2K inpaint to 1254 px).
  • New planners. Gemini 3.8 Flash (Google's strongest Flash, same introductory price as 3.7) and GPT-6 Astra, available but not recommended for Brut's workloads: powerful and expensive, and it acts for Marcel only through OpenRouter for now. Also corrected: Gemini 3.7 Flash estimates had used Google's batch rate, half the real price.
  • One OpenRouter key reaches more. New routes for Nano Banana 2 Lite, Seedance 2.5 and Kling O1, so an OpenRouter-only seat runs the starter graph out of the box.
  • Sequence keeps your sound and your format. Assembled films keep each clip's audio (silent clips get silence, so mixed pools stay in sync, crossfades blend the sound) and adopt the first clip's aspect: vertical reels no longer ship pillarboxed.
  • Run reveals first, then asks. The toolbar's Run no longer generates blindly. Pass one replays every cached, pinned and free step for $0 and marks the nodes whose inputs changed with a needs a run badge (their last result stays on the face); if anything would bill, one dialog itemizes it with the estimate: Generate, or keep the cached state. Every generation node also shows its last result the moment a graph opens, so a reopened graph never looks empty.
  • Sketchpad, from the first exe round: the brush size/softness gesture works with a pen (no pointer lock, the Windows press-and-hold ring is harmless), the brush circle previews at the canvas center while a slider is dragged, a readout next to the pressure boxes shows what the last stroke sent (pen · 78 %, or mouse · no pressure when the tablet driver sends mouse events), and images decode fully before the first preview.
  • Polish: the inpaint mask editor got the Layers-editor feel (wheel zoom at the cursor, middle-drag pan, a brush cursor, [ and ] to size); Ctrl+C on a node with an image on the clipboard pastes the node again; the per-kind frame colors were dropped for a calmer canvas.
1.1.12026-08-31

The Director: the whole cinema crew in one call

In hard field testing on a real competition project, the four-agent chain lost a head-to-head against a single well-briefed model, so the planning side of the cinema pipeline was rebuilt around the winner; everything downstream is untouched.

  • The Director node. One agent, one call: it analyzes your stills and writes the concept, the look and the executable shot plan, with a real cinematic arc and no two shots alike. Brief and Style menus, People and Audio dials, shots and runtime. Your own brief and style arrive on labeled wires (saveable as Prompt presets): a wired brief composes with the menu and wins on conflict. Every clip carries a one-line ambient sound note for video models that produce audio.
  • The proof sheet. Before any keyframe money, render the whole film as one numbered contact sheet, about twenty cents. Select the shots that don't inspire you and hit Reinvent: one planning call rewrites just those, every kept clip stays untouched to the byte. Re-render, loop, then pin the sheet you validate: a pinned sheet survives plan edits and new takes, and every past sheet stays in its history.
  • Sheet-directed keyframes. The validated proof sheet rides into every invented-shot keyframe render as the composition and grade reference: what you approved is what gets produced, and a re-rolled sheet honestly invalidates unpinned keyframes. Field-proven combo: GPT Image 2 for the proof, Nano Banana Pro at 2K for the frames.
  • New takes, board-wide. One button on the Storyboard re-rolls every generated keyframe at once (after a model switch, or when none of them sing), behind one itemized confirm. Pinned, wired and grounded cards are kept; old takes stay in each card's history.
  • Production dials. An Advanced panel on the Director: camera (RED, ARRI, Sony Venice, Blackmagic, IMAX, 35mm and 16mm film), lenses, lighting, composition, subject emphasis and movement energy, each a locked instruction the plan must obey.
  • Four new looks join the style library: Mediterranean Sun, Overcast Delicate, Mysterious Reveal, Ecstatic Life.
  • The Cinema Director template replaces Cinema Crew in the library: ten nodes from stills to a 4K cut, preset to the proven combo, video provider left to you. The four-agent crew (Scenarist, Artistic Director, DoP, Script Supervisor) is now legacy: hidden from the node browser, but every existing graph keeps loading and running exactly as before.
  • Polish: crew plan faces read as documents (the film's title, Review and Re-plan up front, raw JSON behind a toggle), collapsible node sections, and a day's worth of field fixes.
1.1.02026-08-30

Two new video models, a review desk, and a character lab

Everything since 1.0.9, in one installer. The largest video update Brut has had.

  • Seedance 2.5 (BytePlus): clips from 4 to 30 seconds, up to 30 reference images addressed by name in the prompt, first and last frame, 1080p. Frame runs follow the start frame's aspect; a start frame with references switches to the keyframe-reference mode the provider requires. Seedance 2.0 stays in the menu for 4K. Both generations keep their own Refine presets.
  • Gemini Omni Flash: 3 to 10 second clips with sound, on the Gemini key you already have. Billing is flat per clip, about a dollar whatever the length, so the default asks for 10 seconds. Both models live in Image → Video and Film Shots.
  • Generated end frames on the Storyboard. Any card can generate its end keyframe: the crew's embedded writer drafts the prompt from the clip's start frame plus the reference stills it picks from your board, and you edit it before anything is billed. Every prompt a card owns (clip, keyframe, end frame) has its own plug.
  • Mute and tick boxes. ✕ on a Storyboard card mutes it: it stays on the board, greyed, and comes back with one click. Film Shots rows carry tick boxes, so you send exactly the clips you mean to pay for; the Σ row is the master box.
  • Review, on every crew node. The Scenarist, Artistic Director, DoP and Supervisor documents open as documents: headline, prose, style bible, shot cards. Edit any field in place, leave margin notes on a section or a shot, and Revise applies your notes in one planning call, on the node's own model. Invented cards have an anchors editor, and a plan that counted the stills from 1 instead of 0 heals itself.
  • Storyboard keyframe history. Every past take of a card's keyframe is browsable (the 🕘 button) and re-pinnable, conformed to the film's current aspect for free.
  • Multi-Shot V2. The planner used to reach for the same props on every set. Now each run draws a hand of cards from a scenario's deck (a Surprise dial: Classic, Fresh, Crazy), you can redraw the hand or pick cards yourself, and a studio memory of recent motifs keeps the next set from repeating the last one. The planner model is selectable per node, with its reasoning dial. Climate scenarios are retired: climate is a card now, not a scenario. The node's height follows its content.
  • Character Lab, the first built-in composite: wire a character image in, get an expression grid, a full-body studio portrait and an orthographic turnaround sheet out. The character presets anchor identity to the wired image instead of re-describing it in words. Closed composites show their results on the card.
  • Compare node: any two wires, side by side, one click into the wipe slider. ↻ re-roll on every generation node takes another shot without touching the cache; the previous take stays in history.
  • The canvas. One resize language everywhere: a ghost corner grip on hover, no more frames. Conform is a board (2 to 5 per row). Drag an image off any node's preview straight onto the canvas or into an Image Input. The wheel zooms over boards that don't overflow. Box-select catches wires. Marcel places new nodes next to what they connect to, never buried under existing work.
  • Refine gains a model select (Gemini 3.7 Flash by default). The Kling prompt preset keeps to your words and honors the energy you asked for.
  • Catalog: Gemini 3.7 Flash and Claude Opus 5 in the planning roster, GPT-5.6 price cuts applied, GPT Image 2 native prices measured and pinned per size and quality (no more "$?" on the 4K tiers).
  • Fixes from the field: a dropped connection now reads as a network error with the recovery spelled out instead of a blank ReadError; planner replies that arrive slightly malformed are repaired instead of failing; crew-node selects no longer overflow the card; Scenarist shot counts display truthfully; a board wired from a closed composite displays its images.
1.0.92026-07-28

Draw on the canvas

The canvas became a studio wall, and organizing a big graph stopped being a chore.

  • Sticky notes, stamps, pen and text. Notes with light markdown, four fonts and five colors (Marcel reads them as context). Emoji stamps for the keeper and the work-in-progress. A pen that honors Wacom pressure and smooths strokes. Text labels for titling regions. All free, all local, all saved with the graph.
  • The ring menu. Right-click empty canvas: every organize tool under your hand, and the center flips it to your most-used nodes and recent stamps. Press, drag onto a slice, release.
  • Groups with identity: six frame colors, labels readable zoomed all the way out, and a frame adopts any node dropped inside it. Zoom out for real: big canvases fit on screen. Node search is ranked ("fil" finds Film Shots first).
  • Right-click any image or clip → Reveal in Explorer. Outputs belong to the project that was active when they were generated; this is how you find them.
  • Higgsfield routes: 24 job types wired from an authenticated recon of the real parameter sets, so the subscription route falls back cleanly when a native key is missing. Routed video nodes now hide the knobs a route doesn't declare instead of dying on them.
1.0.872026-07-26

Point at things and say what changes

Two new nodes that work the way clients already brief you: markup on a picture.

  • Annotate & Correct. Draw arrows, boxes, circles, lassos and numbered points on an image, write what changes at each mark, attach a RAL color chip (all 216 classic colors, or eyedrop one from the image) or a reference image per mark. One run applies every marked edit. Live-verified on Nano Banana 2, Pro and GPT Image 2.
  • Layers. Photoshop-style masked compositing, local and free: stack a generation over your original, paint the mask (soft brush, opacity, pen pressure, the shortcuts your hands know) and keep every untouched pixel of the source. Generative "nothing else changed" is approximate; Layers makes it exact.
  • The correction loop. "Correct result" annotates a result in one click; "Reconcile with input" paints the original back where a generation overshot.
  • Per-project budget caps: a soft or hard dollar limit, live spend in the toolbar, the line on every paid confirm.
  • Settings, redesigned: provider cards in three tabs, each with an on/off switch. Flip a native key off and your aggregator routes take over.
  • Marcel's taste interview, re-runnable any time, with clickable style chips; Claude 5 reasoning and thinking dials on both his voices.
  • Nano Banana 2 Lite replaces the legacy banana for text-to-image (about $0.034 per render); new Gemini planning models, faster default at the same price.
  • One lightbox everywhere: the magnifier pages any node's whole image set with the arrow keys, and the Clip Board plays your film in it.
  • Beta fixes: keyframes on routed keys, Multi-Shot custom notes, savable prompt presets, Log out buttons for Kling and Higgsfield, modals that survive a stray click, oversized images on OpenRouter, and no more blank error messages.
1.0.82026-07-23

The whole studio on a single key

  • One OpenRouter key now runs almost all of Brut: images (the Nano Banana family with real size and aspect menus), video (Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 incl. Fast and Lite, Seedance 2.0, end frames included), every planning model, and Marcel. A working studio in about two minutes, for those who want simplicity over the last cent.
  • Native keys stay first class: cheapest per generation, every knob, and they take over automatically the moment you add them.
  • Routing is never silent. Routed nodes show a "runs via …" chip, and OpenRouter runs record the exact billed amount on the ledger, not an estimate.
  • Inpaint on aggregator keys for the Nano Banana models; GPT Image 2 via fal and Replicate with each backend's honest size menu.
  • New guide: which setup fits you.
1.0.72026-07-22

Upscalers and routes

  • Upscale Image: Magnific (Creative, Precision V2) and Topaz Labs (Gigapixel and generative models), with savable presets, a live cost estimate, batch upscaling, and a before/after wipe with wheel-zoom in the magnifier.
  • Upscale Video: Topaz Proteus, Rhea and Gaia-CG in the cloud with an itemized confirm before any spend, HD/4K/8K targets, audio kept. And Topaz Video AI, local: if the desktop app is installed, upscales run on your own GPU, free, and the clip never leaves the machine.
  • Routes: a model with no native key can run through an aggregator you subscribe to (fal, Higgsfield, Replicate, OpenRouter for text). Native first, always; the route is flagged on the node and on every ledger line.
  • The Cinema Crew template ends in a finish-in-4K tail.
1.0.42026-07-20

Invented shots

  • The cinema crew can invent. A shot no longer has to be grounded in one of your stills: the Scenarist may invent it, the Storyboard generates its keyframe (per card, pinnable, re-rollable), and one clip animates one keyframe. Stills are reused with discipline, never twice in a row.
  • The film's aspect ratio is a dropdown on the Storyboard; switching it re-conforms every keyframe for free.
  • The activity rail: tools and panels moved to the canvas's left edge, with a categorized node browser (search, hover cards, drag to place) and a new icon set on every node.
  • Text → Text node: a free-form ask to any planning model, editable answer, re-askable.
  • Node clipboard (copy, paste, duplicate with wires), Marcel's prompt cache kept warm for an hour between turns.
1.0.32026-07-19

Kling subscription from a bare install

  • Two buttons set up the Kling (subscription) provider: Install Kling CLI fetches Kling's official client (a one-time ~1 MB download from the npm registry, integrity-checked) and Log in to Kling opens the browser sign-in. No Node, no terminal.
  • The same setup card lives in the onboarding wizard and in Settings; validation errors are visible instead of a dead button.
1.0.12026-07-17

The first installer

  • One .exe. Per-user install, no admin rights, engine and runtime bundled. A tray icon shows engine status; the engine log lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Brut\logs.
  • Beta delivery: approved seats get an email and download from getbrut.app/download after signing in.
  • Marcel, complete: chat grounded in your project's timeline, acting on the canvas (every paid action stops at an itemized confirm), ambient narration behind a four-level Assist slider, a taste profile he proposes and you approve.
  • Spend ledger panel, unread badge on Marcel, hosted sign-in with a 30-day offline grace so the app never bricks without a connection.
