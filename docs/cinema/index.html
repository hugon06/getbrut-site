The Cinema Sessions

Brut's most ambitious workflow: turn a handful of stills into a finished, cut sequence — planned by a crew of AI agents, shot on the video provider of your choice, and assembled locally with ffmpeg, for exactly the bill it showed you.

Stills → a crew that plans the film → a storyboard you edit → clips shot behind a confirm gate → a sequence cut on your machine. You stay in control at every step.

#The pipeline

Conform → Reference Board → Director (plan + proof sheet)
        → Storyboard (keyframes) → Film Shots
        → Clip Board → Sequence → Preview

Every arrow is a wire. The Director passes a plan document (JSON on a text wire); the boards are where you review and curate.

Don't build this by hand the first time: the whole chain ships as the Cinema Director template — File ▾ → Browse library… → Cinema Director. It arrives pre-wired with the field-proven defaults (invented-first reel brief, proof sheets on GPT Image 2, keyframes on Nano Banana Pro at 2K); you add stills, judge the proof, pick your video provider, and run. Let's walk what it does.

#1 · Prepare the stills

Start from your renders — an Image In node (or a Multi-Shot contact sheet you've curated). Run them through Conform to a single aspect ratio (video wants consistency), cropping each to keep the right part of the frame.

Wire the conformed stills into a Reference Board. This makes the pool visible and numbered — and the tile number is the still number the crew will refer to. Reorder to taste; the numbers are the contract.

#2 · The Director plans the film

One Director node. It plays the whole crew in a single call — concept, look and executable shot plan from one model that holds every shot in its head at once, which is exactly what keeps a film varied: the arc is distributed across the shots, lenses and framing change shot to shot, and no still is milked twice in a row. Pick a Brief and a Style, set shots and runtime, choose how much human presence you want, and wire it straight into the Storyboard. Every clip also gets a one-line ambient sound note for video models that produce audio.

Before spending on keyframes, render the Director's 🎬 proof sheet: one cheap image of the whole film as a numbered contact sheet, all frames in one grade. Select the frames that don't inspire you, ✎ Reinvent rewrites only those clips (everything else code-locked), re-render for cents, loop — then 📌 pin the sheet you validate. The pinned sheet rides into every keyframe generation as the composition and grade reference — what you approved is what gets produced.

Pick the Director's LLM — Gemini, GPT or Claude — on your own key. The plan is editable text: tweak a line and it flows downstream. Don't read the raw JSON — the node's 📖 Review opens the plan as an actual document: edit it in place for free, or leave 💬 margin notes and let Revise apply them (details).

The four-agent crew (Scenarist / Artistic Director / DoP / Script Supervisor) is legacy since 1.1.1: the Director demonstrably plans better films in one call, so the crew nodes are hidden from the node browser. Graphs that already use them keep loading and running exactly as before — details.

#Invented shots — the flourish

Your stills define the project's identity, not the shot list. The Director invents most compositions — a detail close-up, a reveal, subtle human presence, the pull-back ending — each anchored on the stills that carry its materials, with a written keyframe prompt already styled to the film's look.

The rule never loosens, it moves one level: one clip = one keyframe. An invented shot's start frame is generated (image-to-image on its anchor stills — and the validated proof sheet — with the identity clause appended, conformed to the film's aspect) before any video runs. An invented shot is never quietly sent as text-to-video — without an approved keyframe it simply refuses to roll.

Want the full reel behavior? The Director's "Invent every shot" switch makes the stills references only — no shot animates one directly, every start frame is generated. Off, the brief decides shot by shot.

#3 · The storyboard — your edit bay

The Storyboard turns the plan into shot cards: per-clip start/end frame swaps with thumbnails, editable and lockable prompts, add/reorder shots, and ✕ mutes a card (greyed, kept, ↺ to bring it back) rather than deleting it. Change a line here and only that shot re-renders. It emits concrete image paths, so the next node just needs the plan wire.

Behind the scenes the planning models are told the stills are numbered 1..N (models naturally count images from 1) and Brut translates their answers back to the board's 0-based numbers before anything is stored — so the plan you see always matches the board's tiles. A plan written under the old numbering (a cached or hand-pasted one whose last number equals the number of stills) is still renumbered automatically and noted in the Activity panel; a genuinely wrong anchor still fails loudly on its card, and 📖 Review on the Director lets you rewrite a shot's anchors by hand.

Invented shots arrive frameless. Each shows its keyframe prompt and a 🎨 Generate keyframe button — pick the image model, resolution and (on GPT Image) quality once at the top of the board (the pending count and per-frame price sit next to it and follow your picks; keyframes are cents, clips are dollars). Generated keyframes cache on their prompt + anchors (an unchanged card never re-pays), ↻ re-rolls a fresh take, 📌 pins one against later prompt edits, 🕘 opens the card's history — every take it ever generated, from the project ledger — so a re-roll never loses anything: pin any past keyframe back as the start frame, free. Wiring any image into the card's socket overrides everything. Prefer to ground an invented shot on a real still after all? Just pick one in its start selector.

The film's aspect is your call. The board's aspect dropdown (default: whatever the DoP wrote) conforms every keyframe to the format you choose and stamps it into the emitted plan. Switching aspect later doesn't re-bill: the raw render is kept and simply re-cropped, free.

Batch it. ▶ on the Storyboard (or the 🎨 Generate N button) renders every pending keyframe behind one itemized confirmation — shot list, per-frame price, total. The per-card 🎨 stays for surgical re-work. Declining isn't an error: the plan flows on and Film Shots simply blocks the frameless clips.

#End frames, on demand

A clip's end frame used to come only from your stills. Now every card's end selector offers three states: none, a still of the board, or 🎨 generate. Pick 🎨 and the card grows a second frame block to the right of the start frame — same image box, same in-socket and out-plug, same tools — for a generated end frame that continues the start frame: it is rendered image-to-image from that start frame (a still, a keyframe or a wired image) as the frame being continued, with a continuity clause and the identity clause appended, conformed to the film's aspect. Where the camera arrives is now yours to direct, for grounded and invented shots alike.

The prompt for that destination is written by the crew: an end-frame writer embedded in the Storyboard sees the actual start frame image, the clip's shot plan (the move, the subject motion, the atmosphere), where that start frame came from, and the reference board — and drafts the frozen final frame of that one move. Because a reveal or a pull-back shows what lies outside the start frame, the writer also picks the reference stills that show what the move reveals (at most three): they ride along as grounded context — the start frame stays image 1, the stills are "the same place, use them for what enters the frame, never for composition" — so the destination is extrapolated from your real project, not invented. It borrows the LLM (and reasoning dial) of the crew node feeding the plan wire — the DoP's or Script Supervisor's brain — one planning call, cents, ledgered like the crew's. Nothing renders from a prompt you haven't seen: ✎ or 🎨 on the end block opens a review — the start frame beside the drafted prompt, editable in place, ✎ re-drafts, and the writer's still picks as toggleable thumbnails (tick more, untick any) — and Generate renders. Cancel keeps the text and renders nothing. ↻ re-rolls a rendered end frame, 📌 pins it (it then survives prompt edits and a re-rolled start frame — otherwise a new start honestly orphans its end, since the end continues it), 🕘 opens the card's end-frame history. Wiring any image into the end block's socket overrides everything, and the block's out-plug carries the resolved end frame wherever you want it. Every prompt a card owns has a plug, too, in a labeled plug bar at the card's bottom: prompt in / prompt out, 🎨 keyframe prompt on invented shots, ⇥ end prompt on armed cards — so any of them can feed a Refine or a Text Board, and you can read which is which.

▶ on the board covers end frames too: after the start keyframes, one review lists every armed card at once — drafts filled in, each editable — and one Generate renders them all. An armed end frame that isn't rendered yet blocks that clip in Film Shots, exactly like a missing start keyframe: it is never sent without it. And when the video model has no end-frame input (only some do — Kling 2.5+/3.0, Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.0 among them), the clip still runs from its start frame, but the Film Shots table says so on that row — never silently. Budget-wise, an end frame is a second keyframe on that card (cents) plus one text call.

Look at your frames. Click any card's image to magnify it full-screen, then browse the whole board like a gallery with ← / → (arrows or the on-screen ‹ ›); Esc closes. And every card has an output plug on its right edge carrying that clip's resolved start frame — the generated keyframe, the wired image, or the grounded still — so you can route a frame anywhere: into an Upscale Image for a delivery-res still, a Compose, a Preview. A card whose keyframe is still pending delivers nothing (never a wrong frame). The card's prompt has its own plug too, on the bottom-right — the final clip prompt, overrides and wired prompts applied — so a line of the DoP's wording can be reused or refined anywhere (a Refine, a Text Board, another card's prompt socket). And any card image can simply be dragged onto the canvas: it lands as a ready-filled Image In — detach a keyframe as the frozen base of a new exploration, safe from later re-plans.

#4 · Shoot — behind the confirm gate

Wire the Storyboard into Film Shots. It prices every clip — model, duration, resolution, cost — and shows an itemized confirmation. Nothing generates until you approve the total; cancel a line and it updates. A tick box per row chooses what gets sent (untick = kept in the table, greyed, not sent — render a few, judge, then the rest). Cached and pinned clips cost $0. Then it fans out, staggered to respect rate limits, with per-clip re-roll / ✓✗ / pin.

#5 · Review & cut

The Clip Board plays every clip — keep the good, ✗ the misses, ↻ re-roll one. Route the keepers into a Sequence node, which assembles them into one mp4 with local ffmpeg — cuts or crossfades, clip audio kept, vertical reels stay vertical, free. Drop a Preview on the end.

Want it in 4K? Route a hero clip (or the assembled film) through Upscale Video — Topaz Proteus or Gaia CG, audio kept, itemized confirm before any spend. The shipped Cinema Director template already ends with this finish-in-4K tail (provider unset — the money choice stays yours).

#Make it reusable

The Cinema Director template is the reusable starting point — and any tuned chain of yours (a Director with your house style and dials, a Storyboard with your keyframe model) can be selected and collapsed into a composite or saved as your own template: one library item, every film starts warm.

Why it holds together: grounding is enforced in code, not prose — one clip to one keyframe (a still of yours, or a frame generated from your stills and the validated proof sheet), still indices reconciled automatically, invented shots blocked until their keyframe exists, an anti-shake stability clause on every clip. The plan you approve is the film you get.
