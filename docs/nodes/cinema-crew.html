The Director & the Storyboard

The cinema planning nodes: they read your stills and pass a plan document (JSON on a text wire) toward Film Shots. Planning runs on the LLM you choose — Gemini, GPT or Claude — on your own key; on models with a reasoning dial (GPT-5.6, details; Claude Sonnet 5 / Opus 4.8, details) a Reasoning select appears — deeper thinking for harder plans, billed as output tokens. For the end-to-end story, see the Cinema Sessions walkthrough.

The chain is: stills → Director → Storyboard → Film Shots. (The original four-agent crew — Scenarist, Artistic Director, DoP, Script Supervisor — is legacy, kept at the bottom of this page.)

#The Director — the whole crew in one node

One agent that holds the entire film in its head: it analyzes your stills, writes the concept, the look and the executable shot plan in a single call, and wires straight into the Storyboard. Because one model plans every shot with full awareness of all the others, the plan comes out varied by construction: an arc distributed across the shots (atmospheric hook, materials, reveal, hero frames, a pull-back ending), focal length, framing scale, camera height and movement changing shot to shot, and a hard cap on how often any still may be re-used.

Its dials: a Brief (what film) and a Style (what look) from the same libraries the crew uses, the shot count and runtime, a People dial (none / subtle / lively), and an Audio switch that has every clip carry a one-line ambient sound note (folded into generation for video models that actually produce audio — a silent model never gets sound words). The plan it writes also carries a concept paragraph: the film's emotional tone, arc, lighting mood and pacing, readable and editable in 📖 Review like everything else.

Advanced dials — locked production choices. The Advanced disclosure exposes six craft dials: Camera (RED digital / ARRI / Sony Venice / Blackmagic / IMAX / 35mm / 16mm film), Lenses (clean primes / anamorphic / vintage / long-lens compression), Lighting, Composition, Emphasis and Movement (contemplative → kinetic). Each pick is a fixed clause the Director must obey — it overrides any conflicting style language — and every dial defaults to Director's choice, which adds nothing. The vocabularies are data (cinema.json), so the studio can grow them.

The proof sheet is always ONE image. 🎬 Proof renders the whole film as a single contact sheet at the price on the bar — after a targeted revise, the same button re-renders it (an unchanged plan replays from cache, free; ↻ New take forces a fresh render of the same plan, paying again). 🕘 opens every sheet the film ever rendered, from the project ledger, and 📌 pins one — a pinned sheet stays the film's validated sheet (and keyframe reference) through plan edits and new takes, until unpinned. The node's Setup, plan and Proof sheet sections all collapse to keep the card compact.

Your own brief and style are wires, not notes. The node has two labeled text inlets — brief (optional) and style (optional) — made for a Prompt node (whose text is saveable as a preset, or refined through a Refine). A wired brief composes with the select: the canned entry sets the genre, your wire carries the project's specifics, and where they conflict the wire wins — the node says "⇄ wired in" under the select so the precedence is never invisible. For a fully custom film, set the select to None and let the wire direct alone. The Notes box is just notes: extra guidance, always honored, never secretly the brief.

The proof sheet — see the whole film for cents. Below the plan, the Director offers 🎬 Proof: ONE image generation renders the entire film as a numbered contact sheet — every frame, one shared grade — from your stills and the plan's prompts (pick the image model, size and quality on the bar; cheap settings are fine, this is for your eyes). Judge it, then ✗ the frames that fail (the chips match the sheet's number badges) and hit ✎ Reinvent — one planning call rewrites only the rejected clips with genuinely new compositions; every kept clip is code-locked back verbatim, whatever the model returns. Re-render the sheet for cents and loop until the film reads right. The spend ladder is the point: ~1¢ of plan → ~6¢ of proof → keyframes → clips, and you never pay the next tier until the previous one convinced you.

The validated sheet then directs the keyframes. When a Storyboard downstream generates an invented shot's keyframe, the Director's proof sheet rides along as a reference with a code-locked instruction: render that frame of the sheet at full resolution — its composition, light and grading — using the reference stills for the building's true geometry and materials. The sheet is part of the keyframe's cache identity, so re-rolling the sheet honestly invalidates unpinned keyframes: what you validated is what gets produced. (The film's style bible is also folded into every keyframe render, sheet or no sheet, so independently generated frames read as one film. The sheet itself is never cropped or reused as a frame — its resolution is for human eyes only.)

It emits the exact same plan document as the legacy DoP chain, so everything downstream is identical: the Storyboard, keyframes, end frames, Film Shots, Review and Revise.

#The legacy crew — Scenarist, Artistic Director, DoP, Script Supervisor

Legacy since 1.1.1. The Director plans measurably better films in one call than the four-agent chain (one model holding every shot in its head keeps a film varied; the chain's later agents were contractually barred from fixing a weak shot list). The crew nodes are therefore hidden from the node browser — but they remain fully functional: every saved graph that uses them keeps loading and running, Review/Revise included. New films should start from the Director (or the Cinema Director library template).

#Scenarist

Writes the treatment — the story and beat structure — from your stills. You pick a brief (or custom, where your notes are the brief). Its contract enforces one shot = one still, so grounding stays honest.

It may also invent shots — compositions no still contains (detail close-ups, reveals, human presence, pull-back endings; the Cinematic Reel (flourish) brief leans into this). An invented shot names the stills that anchor it and carries a written keyframe prompt; Brut generates that missing frame from your stills on the Storyboard before any video runs, so the identity never drifts. Still reuse is disciplined by contract — a still may directly ground at most two shots, never back to back — and the "Invent every shot" switch goes all the way: stills become references only, every start frame generated.

#Artistic Director

Sets the look: a style bible and per-shot art direction — palette, lighting, mood. Choose a style (or custom). It builds on the Scenarist's treatment. On invented shots it also styles the keyframe prompt (palette, light, lens character) — composing the frame is exactly its job.

#DoP (Director of Photography)

Turns the treatment into an executable shot plan — the camera language, motion, and which still grounds each shot, as a concrete list of clips with durations. This is the plan Film Shots executes.

#Script Supervisor

The continuity pass: harmonizes speed, light and palette across all the clips so the sequence feels like one film, not a pile of clips. Grounding is code-locked so it can't drift off the stills.

#Storyboard — the edit bay

Not an agent but the crew's review surface. The Storyboard turns the plan into an editable board of shot cards: per-clip start/end frame swaps with thumbnails, editable and lockable prompts, add/reorder shots, dynamic position badges. It emits concrete image paths, so Film Shots downstream only needs the plan wire.

✕ mutes, it never deletes. A muted card stays on the board, greyed and badged muted, out of the film: its keyframes, pins and prompts are kept, the position badges renumber around it, and ↺ on the card (or ↺ N muted in the header) brings it back in place. Only a hand-added shot can be deleted for good (🗑, once muted). The same discipline as the Shot Board's ✗: a clip you cut is a clip you can still see.

Still numbers heal themselves. The stills are numbered from 0, but a model that counts them sometimes counts from 1, and every reference in its plan lands one too high (the last one pointing past the board: "anchor still #15 isn't wired in, only 15 stills"). That signature is unambiguous, so Brut renumbers such a plan in code, at the agent that wrote it and again on the Storyboard against the wired stills, and says so in the Activity panel. Anything that isn't that exact pattern is left alone and still fails loudly on the card. Never a silent clamp.

Invented shots arrive frameless with a 🎨 Generate keyframe button: an image-to-image render on the shot's anchor stills (identity clause appended, conformed to the film's aspect — cents, priced on the board next to the image-model picker). Keyframes cache on their prompt + anchors, ↻ re-rolls, 📌 pins, 🕘 opens the card's history — every keyframe it ever generated, from the project ledger, any past take re-pinnable as the start frame for free — a wired image overrides, and the start selector can still ground the shot on a real still instead. An invented shot with no keyframe blocks that clip in Film Shots — it is never silently sent as text-to-video.

The board also owns the film's aspect ratio: a dropdown (defaulting to the DoP's format) that conforms every keyframe and travels in the emitted plan. Changing it re-crops the kept raw renders for free — no regeneration.

▶ on the board batch-renders every pending keyframe behind one itemized confirmation (count, per-frame price, total); the per-card 🎨 stays for one-offs. Its companion ↻ New takes re-rolls every generated keyframe at once — fresh takes on the current model, each paying again, one itemized confirm — for a model switch or a general do-over; 📌 pinned, wired and grounded cards are kept, and old takes stay pinnable in each card's 🕘 history. A prompts on/off switch declutters the cards, and the whole pre-wired chain ships as the Cinema Crew library template (File ▾ → Browse library…).

End frames, on demand. Each card's end selector is none · a still · 🎨 generate. Pick 🎨 and the card grows a second frame block to the right — same image box, own in-socket and out-plug, own tools — for a generated end frame that continues the start frame: rendered image-to-image from that start frame as image 1, with the reference stills that show what the move reveals as grounded context (never for composition), continuity + identity clauses appended, conformed to the film's aspect. The crew writes its prompt: an end-frame writer embedded in the Storyboard (borrowing the LLM and reasoning dial of the crew node on the plan wire) sees the start frame image, the clip's shot plan and the reference board, drafts the frozen final frame of that one move and picks the stills (at most three) that ground what lies outside the start frame; ✎ / 🎨 open a review — start frame beside the editable draft, ✎ re-drafts, the still picks as toggleable thumbnails, Generate renders — so nothing renders from a prompt you haven't read. ↻ re-rolls, 📌 pins (surviving prompt edits and a re-rolled start; otherwise a new start orphans its end), 🕘 = the end frame's history. ▶ on the board reviews every armed card in one modal after the start keyframes. An armed end frame still pending blocks its clip downstream; a video model without an end-frame input runs the clip from the start alone and the Film Shots table says so on that row.

Every frame block is also a source: the plug on its image edge carries that frame — the start frame (keyframe, wired image or grounded still) or the end frame — and a pending frame delivers nothing; the labeled plug bar at the card's bottom carries the clip's final prompt (overrides and wired prompts applied; in on the left, out on the right), an invented shot's keyframe prompt and the validated end prompt, for reuse or refinement anywhere. Card images can be dragged straight onto the canvas to detach into a ready-filled Image In.

#📖 Review — read it like a document, direct it like a director

The raw JSON on the node face is for the wire, not for you. Every crew node has a 📖 Review button that opens the document as a document: the title as a headline, the analysis as prose, the style bible as labeled sections, every shot a numbered card with its prompt, camera, duration and grounding badges.

Two verbs live in that modal, with two costs:

  • Edit — free. Every text field is editable in place. Save writes each field back into the same place in the document, so the structure the downstream chain depends on can never drift; it behaves exactly like hand-editing the node's text, just humane.
  • Anchors are editable too. An invented shot's card shows its anchor stills (the 0-based still numbers its keyframe is built from) as a small list you can rewrite: 0, 3. The field knows how many stills the node is wired to and flags any number the board can't serve, so a stray anchor is a two-second fix instead of a re-plan.
  • 💬 Comment → Revise — one planning call (cents, ledgered). Every section and shot card takes a margin note ("shot 4 slower", "the whole film should feel like dusk"), plus an overall-notes box. Revise with comments hands the agent its own document and your notes; it returns the same document revised — same shot ids, same structure, only what your notes require changed — and the comments clear once addressed. A revision that would break the document contract is rejected and your document stays untouched. Comments survive closing the modal, and the node's Review button shows how many are waiting.

They compose: fix wording directly, comment the conceptual asks, revise — the revision starts from your edited text.

#How the plan flows

Agent output is editable text. Hand-edit a plan and your changes flow downstream while inputs are unchanged; change an input or roll again (🎲) and the agent regenerates over your edits — deliberately. The planner sees your stills (downscaled for the model's request cap; generation always gets full resolution) and returns strict JSON, whose analysis is surfaced in the Activity panel.

Grounding is code, not prose. One clip animates one keyframe — a still of yours, or a frame generated from your stills; the DoP's still-index references are reconciled in code, invented shots are blocked until their keyframe exists, and an anti-shake stability clause is appended to every clip — so the plan you approve is the film you get.
