Scene & Camera (3D)

Bring a 3D model onto the canvas, walk around it, frame physical cameras, and take a clay screenshot of each view. The screenshot lands on a Camera node as an ordinary picture: wire it into Image → Image or the Clay → Photoreal workflow and a model renders the photograph, exactly as with a viewport grab from your 3D tool. Both nodes are free and local, and the model never leaves your machine.

#The Scene node

  • What it takes: a GLB or glTF, an OBJ (with its MTL and textures), an FBX, an STL or a PLY. Drop the file on the node, or on an empty spot of the canvas (a Scene node appears there), or click Choose… and pick it on disk. Picking an OBJ with Choose… brings its MTL and the textures it names along (a texture name with spaces in it included), and picking an FBX brings the textures it names that sit in the same folder; when you drop a model, drop its MTL and textures with it. A file of another kind is refused with one line on the node, and so is a GLB that did not copy whole or one saved with mesh compression (export it again without compression).
  • What it shows: the file's name, then the model itself: a picture of the last view the navigator showed, in the view you left it on: with the model's materials when you left the navigator on Textured (and the model carries materials), else in clay under the sun. Hover the name for its size and what it holds (meshes, materials, triangles; an FBX gets its counts the first time you open it). The picture is made when you take a screenshot, when you close the navigator, and the first time you open a Scene node that has none yet; until then the empty box says so, and a click on it opens the navigator. A click on the picture opens the navigator on the view it shows (through the camera it was taken from, when it was), and a right click gives the usual picture menu (Reveal in Explorer, Copy file path). There is no 3D view on the node itself: the canvas stays fast with many of them. Under it, on the plug's row, a short line gives the model's faces, its file size and its units.
  • The cameras: one row of small pictures under the model, each camera's last screenshot with its name. Click one to look through that camera in the navigator. A line names any file the model asks for that was not found beside it (an MTL, a texture: the model then opens grey there).
  • Choose… (or Replace… once a model is in) and Explore, one row of buttons. Replace… swaps the model and keeps the cameras, the sun and the view; the picture of the old model goes with it. Explore opens the navigator. There is no ▶: nothing runs, the screenshots are taken in the navigator.
  • The plug: scene goes to the Camera nodes. It carries the model and its cameras, never a picture.

#The navigator

A full-screen view of the model on the left, the panel on the right. The view is framed exactly like the screenshot will be. The first time you open one in a session, the frame says Loading the viewer… while the viewer itself loads, then Reading the model….

  • Moving around, the 3ds Max way (Arc): Alt + middle drag orbits, middle drag pans, the wheel zooms toward the cursor, Ctrl + Alt + middle drag zooms smoothly. The left button orbits too (Shift + left drag pans), for a trackpad. The keys of Walk work here as well: W A S D, Q and E move the camera, the view keeping its direction; the point it orbits around moves with it, unless an object is picked (see below), which stays the center of the orbit.
  • Orbit around an object: in Arc, a click (without dragging) on the model picks the object under the cursor, as the file names it (an object made of several materials is still one object). It is outlined, its name shows at the top left of the view, and the view does not move.
    • Arc then orbits around the object's center, as 3ds Max does: the object stays in its place on the screen while the camera turns, even when it sits off the middle.
    • The pick lasts while you look through a camera, walk, or come back to Arc; picking never changes a camera's framing.
    • A click in the empty space or Escape clears it. The outline is only on screen: it never appears in a screenshot or the node's picture.
  • Z frames the picked object, or the whole model when nothing is picked.
  • Walk: from exactly where you stand and where you look, with nothing reset. W and S move along the direction you look (looking down and pressing W goes down), A and D sideways, E straight up and Q straight down, drag to look around, Shift to go faster; the arrow keys work as W A S D. A key held down keeps you moving for as long as you hold it; leaving the window (another app, another tab) stops you where you are. Arc goes back to orbiting.
  • Speed: how fast the keys move, in both modes. It follows the distance to what is in front of you, as in a game editor: close to a wall you move slowly, with open space ahead you move fast, so a site model a kilometer wide still walks at a building's pace near the building. The model's size only sets the top speed: with the whole model in view you move as before, crossing it in about ten seconds at 1×. When nothing is in front of you (the horizon, the sky, a gap between buildings), the ground ahead of you sets it: a walking pace at eye height, faster from high up. The Speed slider multiplies that, from a tenth to ten times, and Shift goes faster still. Each Scene node keeps its own speed; it changes nothing in the cameras or the pictures.
  • Cameras: Add camera makes a camera from the current view and looks through it: navigating now moves that camera. Click a camera in the list (or Look through) to look through it, Free view to leave it. Double-click a name to rename it (its Camera node takes the new name at once), the bin removes the camera (its Camera node keeps its picture, and a camera added later never takes its place on that node).
  • Lock a camera: the lock on a camera's row (the same lock sits on its Camera node) keeps a saved camera from moving. You can still look through a locked camera and Take screenshot to take its picture again. The first move through it (a drag, the wheel, a key, Z) leaves it for the free view, which starts from the camera's own view and lens, and the line at the bottom says so; the camera itself never moves. While you look through it, its lens and frame settings stay in place, greyed. Click the lock again to free it. The lock is saved with the scene and changes nothing in the pictures or what they feed.
  • Lens: Focal length, from 8 to 700 mm; Film back, a list of film formats (36 mm full frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds, medium format); and Film width, the same width set to any value (36 mm is full frame, so 35 mm on 36 mm gives a 54° horizontal view). The focal slider gives most of its travel to the short lenses where most views live and still reaches 700 mm. The lens edited is the camera you look through, or the free view's.
  • Lock verticals: levels the camera and shifts the frame so vertical lines stay vertical, the two-point perspective of architectural photography. The panel shows the shift it applies; a view steeper than 60° cannot be leveled and says so. Shift ↕ and Shift ↔ add a manual lens shift on top.
  • Frame: Portrait, Square and Landscape in one click, or any aspect from the list (16:9, 3:2, 4:3, 1:1, 4:5, 3:4, 2:3, 9:16), and the Size: 1K, 2K or 4K on the long side. The line below gives the screenshot's pixel size (2K at 16:9 = 2048 × 1152).
  • Sun: Azimuth (0 north, 90 east, 180 south) and Elevation (low = long shadows) move the shadows of the clay.
  • View: Fill light (flat even light, no shadows, to find your way around), Clay (grey under the sun, with shadows: what the screenshot renders), Wireframe (the edges of every triangle), Textured (the model's own materials under the same sun).
  • Model: the Units the file was exported in (a GLB is in meters; an OBJ, STL or PLY is guessed from its size, an FBX says its own) and Z up for a model exported lying down. The keys' top speed follows the model's size in its units.
  • Take screenshot, always at the foot of the panel, renders the clay at the frame's size, plus a second picture with the model's materials when the file carries some (Textured picture too). A screenshot of the free view makes a camera of it first. While it renders, the camera list waits; you can keep looking around and adjust the lens, nothing you change is undone when the picture lands.

Sliders move the view live and save when you let go. Every slider of the navigator (Focal length, Film width, the two shifts, the sun's Azimuth and Elevation, Speed) works the same way: double-click the number to type a value (Enter applies, Escape cancels; a value past the range is held to it); while you drag, it snaps to its neutral value as you pass it (0 for the shifts, 35 mm for the focal length, 36 mm for the film width, 225° and 40° for the sun, 1× for Speed); a double-click on the slider or on its name puts it back there. The arrow keys still reach every value. The line at the bottom lists the keys of the current mode. Everything done in one visit of the navigator undoes as one step once it is closed (Ctrl+Z on the canvas); while it is open, undo waits. A visit that only looked around (the free view moved, nothing else) is no step at all, and an undo keeps where the free view looks from.

#The Camera node

One camera of a Scene node and the screenshot taken through it. It appears beside the Scene node, wired to it, the first time you take a screenshot of that camera, below the Camera nodes already there. When the Scene node sits in a frame, the Camera node joins the frame; when it sits inside a closed card, the Camera node goes beside the card, and a card with no scene plug for it gets one, so the wire shows. A new screenshot of the same camera replaces its pictures (a new file, so whatever is wired downstream sees the change and runs again when you ask).

  • What it shows: the camera's name, its lens (focal length, film back, the vertical shift, the aspect and the size), the clay and, when taken, the textured picture.
  • Lock: the lock beside Rename is the camera's own lock, the same one as on its row in the navigator: a locked camera can be looked through and shot again, never moved. Greyed while the node holds no camera of the scene.
  • Rename: double-click the node's title, or click Rename beside Look through. A camera has one name: the Camera node, the Scene node's camera row, the navigator's list and the lines that name the camera all follow. A Camera node that holds no camera yet has nothing to rename: Rename stays greyed until its first screenshot.
  • Look through (greyed until the Scene node holds a model) opens the navigator on this camera, and a screenshot taken there lands on this node. A Camera node added by hand from the node list, or one whose camera was removed from the scene before you open it, opens the free view instead: frame a view and Take screenshot, the picture lands on that node (the line at the bottom of the navigator names it). A camera removed while the navigator is open never hands its node to another camera. A screenshot still rendering when you close the navigator lands on its own Scene node, even if you open another one meanwhile; if that Scene node was removed meanwhile, or now holds another model, the picture is not kept.
  • A copy (Ctrl+D, or Ctrl+C then Ctrl+V) gets its own camera: the Scene node gains a new camera with the view, lens, frame and size the original's had at the moment of the copy, and the copy looks through it. Moving it, changing its lens or taking its screenshot leaves the original alone, and the other way round. Copied together with its Scene node, it belongs to the new Scene node; pasted where its Scene node is not wired, it holds no camera yet and its Look through opens the free view.
  • The plugs: clay (the main one), textured (greyed while it holds no picture: the model carries no materials, or the screenshot was taken without Textured picture too) and draft, greyed and empty for now: renders inside the navigator come later. A greyed plug takes no wire. There is no ▶ on the Camera node: free, nothing to run.
  • An empty plug: any node that generates and is fed by a plug carrying no picture (textured after a clay-only screenshot, a Camera node with no screenshot yet) stops when it runs, before anything is sent and at no cost, with one line naming the camera; a closed card says it once, under the card's own name. An Image → Video never makes its clip from the prompt alone, a Compose never takes its reference as the scene, no node runs on fewer pictures than you wired. What a stopped node feeds waits on it, and ▶ Run neither prices nor sends it.
  • Wherever it is wired: straight in, through reroute dots, through a Reference Board (a board inside a board too) or a Seedance assets node, into a closed card, or into a Storyboard card whose frame it uses. One camera not shot yet on a board stops what the board feeds, even when the other cameras there have their pictures. A Storyboard card whose start or end frame is wired from such a camera shows that frame empty, with the camera's name, and never puts its plan still or a keyframe in the camera's place; a card with no wire keeps its still. A camera not shot yet among the Storyboard's stills stops what the cards and the plan feed: without its picture, every card after it would take the wrong still. The cards that use a still show that frame empty with the camera's name, and the board makes no keyframe until the camera has its picture. A card whose start frame waits on a camera holds its end frame too: the keyframe bar counts and prices only what a run makes, and says which end frames wait and on which camera. Hover an empty frame to read what to do. The board's status says which cards wait on which camera.
  • What still runs: a Conform on crop or pad (free; on extend it stops like the others), and a node holding a pinned result (📌): the pin costs nothing and flows on.

The way to a photograph today: wire clay into Image → Image with a prompt, or into the Clay → Photoreal workflow, as you would a viewport grab. Several cameras, one look: wire every Camera node's clay into the Clay → Photoreal card (or into one Image → Image with Each picture its own render ticked): each camera gets its own render, and the first render sets the materials, the light and the sky of the others, so the building is not reinvented from one view to the next. Wire a photo or a render into the card's reference instead, and that picture is the look of every camera: the step that writes the prompt reads it too, so the materials, the planting, the light and the sky come from the reference, not from the clay or the textured capture.

#Cost and limits

  • Free and local: opening, navigating, taking screenshots and the node's picture cost nothing and call no provider. A screenshot goes to a provider only when you wire it into a node that generates, priced like any picture there.
  • The model is copied into your project, with the files it names, and stays on your machine. Save project as… moves it with those files. A model over 512 MB with its files is refused: reduce it in your 3D tool first.
  • Renders inside the navigator, materials per object, color IDs, depth and the live link with 3ds Max come later.
