Utility nodes

Small, local, free — the glue between generation steps.

#Conform

Brings stills to one aspect ratio before generation, so a set of mixed-ratio renders becomes a clean, consistent batch (video models in particular want a single ratio).

  • Choose a target aspect ratio and a method — crop or pad.
  • Per-image ✂ crop editor: drag an aspect-locked rectangle to choose which part of each still survives the conform (the default is a center cut — architecture rarely sits dead-center). The crop is stored per image; the engine crops the original at full resolution.
  • The stills sit on a resizable board: pick 2, 3, 4 or 5 per row from the n / row selector and drag the node's corner to size the tiles; many stills scroll inside the frame instead of stretching the node into a column.
  • Each image gets its own output plug, so you can route conformed stills individually.
  • Each row's 🔍 opens the lightbox on the conformed result — ←/→ pages the whole set, so you can flip through the batch and check every cut.

Conform runs entirely on your machine (local PIL) — free, never metered.

#Preview

Inspect any output at screen size. Wire an image or video into a Preview node and it shows inline on a resizable frame; the image inlet displays video too. Hover a tile and its 🔍 opens the lightbox — wheel-zoom, and ←/→ pages through everything on the node. When several images are shown, the ‹ › buttons on each tile reorder them. Preview is the "show me the result" node you'll drop at the end of most branches; the full-resolution files live in the project's output folder.

#Compare

Two images, one verdict. Wire any two takes into the A and B inlets — a Gemini attempt against a GPT attempt, an original against an Annotate correction, yesterday's keeper against today's re-roll — and the node shows them side by side, live, without running anything. Hover it and the 🔍 opens the real thing: the before/after wipe slider, both layers sharing one zoom and pan, so you can drag the divider across a facade while zoomed into the brickwork. The slider labels pick up the source nodes' names, so it reads "Image → Image vs Annotate & Correct", not "before vs after".

Compare is a viewing surface, not a processor — free, local, no output port and no ▶ (there is nothing to run; the tiles follow the wires live — route the winner onward from its source node). It's the generalization of the Upscale node's built-in input-vs-result compare: same slider, any two wires you choose.

#History

**Every take a node ever made, on the canvas.** Projects grow hundreds of generations and the good one from Tuesday is somewhere in the outputs folder. Wire any generation node's output into the History board (the **image** inlet takes video nodes too) and the board fills with **every take that node ever produced, newest first**, straight from the project's generation ledger. Wire several nodes in and the board shows **one group per node**, in wire order, each headed by the node's name and id, its take count and what it has cost so far. That is the point of the board: it reads as "what is inside this branch", not as a flat pile.
  • Tiles carry the model, the date, the cost and, where it applies, the shot number of a Multi-Shot take, the card of a Storyboard keyframe or the Director's proof sheet. The prompt sits under the caption and in the tooltip. Every tile shows the take at its own aspect: a 9:16 take is a tall tile, a 16:9 take a wide one, never a crop, so a node that changed format mid-way reads truthfully. A tile's 🔍 opens the Magnifier, and ←/→ page through that node's whole history. Video takes play in place: a still poster at rest, click ▶ to play (one tile at a time, so a board of forty clips stays light).
  • 📌 pins on the source node. The pin is exactly the 🕘 re-pin: the take becomes the source node's active output on every run, free, until you unpin it there or here. A pinned tile wears the amber ring. Multi-Shot tiles pin their own shot, Storyboard tiles their own keyframe. The board itself has no output and never runs anything: pins act on the source, so keep the downstream wires on the source node and use the board to choose.
  • Drag an image tile onto the canvas to detach it as a ready-filled Image Input, the same gesture as dragging any preview out of a node. Right-click a tile for Reveal in Explorer, Copy, Export.
  • Filter by model when a node has run on several, pick the tiles per row, ↻ re-reads the ledger (it also refreshes on its own whenever a tracked node finishes a run).
  • Wires through a composite card or a reroute dot resolve to the node that actually generated: the board always shows the generator's own history.

Free, local, nothing leaves the machine: the ledger is a file in the project folder. The board shows the newest 200 takes per node.

#Note

A sticky note for the canvas — annotate your graph the way you'd annotate a whiteboard: intent for future-you, instructions for a teammate opening the project later, review remarks next to the branch they're about.

Notes aren't nodes — they're placement tools, living in the rail's Organize section (with Stamp, Group and Composite). Click the rail's sticky-note icon and the note rides your cursor: click the canvas to place it at the default size, or click-and-drag to draw its size (Shift places several, Esc or right-click cancels). While you're placing, the nodes underneath freeze — you can drop a note on top of anything.

  • Double-click to write, click elsewhere to finish. Notes support a light markdown: # heading, - bullet, **bold**, *italic*, `code`.
  • Select a note for the toolbar: five sticky colors, four fonts (Sans / Display / Hand / Mono), text sizes from 11 to 256px, bold and italic, left / center / right alignment, plus resize handles.
  • Display order is fixed so annotations always show: stamps over notes over nodes (selecting anything still brings it to the front).
  • Notes have no ports and never execute — free, local, never sent to any provider as part of a run.
  • Notes travel: they're saved in graphs, templates and composites, so a library template can ship its own usage notes. Tidy moves them with the graph — each note keeps its position relative to the node it sits next to.
  • Marcel reads them. A note sitting next to a node is exactly the kind of intent he should see — ask him about the graph and the notes are part of his picture. He can write them too.

#Stamp

A frameless emoji mark — the quickest possible annotation. Click the rail's stamp icon, pick your emoji from the flyout, and it rides your cursor; click to drop it — on empty canvas or right on a node (Shift-click for a trail of them). Drop a ⭐ on the keeper, a 🚧 on the branch under construction, a 💩 on what it deserves. Select a stamp to swap the emoji, resize it by the grip in its corner (like a sticky), or delete it. Free, local, no ports.

#Pen

Draw on the canvas — made for Wacom people (mouse works too). The rail's Pen icon (or the ring's Pen slice) enters draw mode: sketch freely, pen pressure sets the stroke weight, pick a color (theme ink + the five sticky colors) and a width, Alt-click erases a stroke, and Esc or ✓ finishes — the whole session lands as one frameless drawing you can move, resize (it scales as a unit) and delete like any annotation. Free, local, never sent anywhere.

#Text

A clean label — for those who prefer tidy organization over stickies: the same note machinery with the paper removed. No fill, no shadow, just typed words on the canvas — perfect for titling regions of a big graph. The label sizes to its content by default; select it and drag the knob on its left or right edge to set the width (the height always follows the text, so a title never clips; double-click a knob to fit the text again), and the font size is its scale control, up to a poster-sized 256px. It gets the full note toolbar (four fonts, sizes, bold, italic, alignment), and the colors apply to the text itself, with a theme-ink default.

#The ring menu

Right-click any empty spot on the canvas and a radial menu opens under your hand — every organize tool one flick away. The center names the ring you're on and clicking it cycles three states: Organize (note · stamp · pen · text · group · composite · add node · tidy) → your most-used nodes (learned from your own placements) → your recent stamps. Nodes land where you right-clicked; the annotation tools (note, stamp, text) hop onto your cursor so you place — or click-drag-size — them exactly like from the rail. Power move: press the right button, drag onto a slice, release — picked, no clicking. The rail keeps every tool too; the ring is just the fast hand.

That's the whole palette. Back to Nodes & graphs for how they run together, or on to the Providers.
