Image generation

The core image nodes. All of them take a prompt (and, where relevant, images) and produce an image on a provider of your choice.

#Text → Image

Generate an image from a prompt. Pick a provider and model, set the aspect ratio and (on Gemini) the resolution, wire a prompt in, run. This is the starting point of most graphs — see Your first image. A ratio a model does not render at the chosen size is greyed in place with the reason (Ideogram 4.5 has no 21:9 at 1K: pick 2K).

Providers: Gemini (Nano Banana family), OpenAI (GPT Image 2, plus the GPT Image 2.5 and 2.6 pairs, Flare and Sunburst; both pairs unlock two extra quality steps, xhigh and max, on the quality select, and the 2.6 pair adds a Complexity select: how much the model plans before it draws) and Seedream (BytePlus's Seedream 5.0 Pro, Flash and Lite, on the Seedance key: 1K / 1.5K / 2K, Lite 2K / 3K / 4K, from $0.018 per image; you get exactly the size you pick).

Every image node's footer shows its result's resolution (2048 × 1152, · ×4 on a batch) at the bottom left, on the output pin's row, the real pixel size of the file, not the preview's. Every image node's title bar has ▶ (run this node and what it needs; unchanged inputs replay the last result, free), 🕘 (history: every past result, pin one) and ↻ re-roll (a fresh take with the same inputs: bills again, the current take stays in history). See Never pay twice.

#Image → Image

Edit or transform an existing image with a prompt. Wire an Image In (or any upstream image) into the image inlet and a prompt into prompt. Good for variations, retouching, and style changes. A picture wired into reference is the look to follow (see A reference: the look to follow), and several pictures can each get their own render (see Each picture its own render).

Several pictures can be wired into image, in wire order (connect the picture to edit first). What the model does with them differs by family. Nano Banana and GPT Image treat the first wire as the picture being edited and the next ones as references the prompt can name as image 2, image 3: a clay frame first and a portrait second, with the small figure is the man of image 2, renders the frame in that man's likeness (measured on Nano Banana 2.1). Seedream blends several pictures into a new composition (its maker's own term: multi-image blending): no picture is the base and the framing follows the richest one, so the same wiring came back as the portrait reshaped to the frame's aspect, on Pro and on Flash alike. To put a subject at a place in a picture on Seedream, use a marks step on an Adjust node: a box on the place, with the portrait as the mark's reference, puts the subject there.

With nothing wired into image, the node renders the prompt as text-to-image on the same model (when the model offers it) and says so in the activity log; an aspect of auto then falls back to the model's own default aspect, since there is no input to follow.

Which picture is which. Under the inlets, the node lists every wired picture: its thumbnail, the @name it answers to and its role, in the order the request carries them. Write those names in the prompt (the small figure is @man, match the light of @villa-cam03), by hand or through the Prompt node's @ list: Brut tells the model which picture you mean, on every model. Names come from the Image In (a picture is named after its file or the node that made it until you type a name); a picture nothing names reads unnamed · @picture2, and @picture2 works too. The same legend sits on Adjust, Compose and the video nodes.

When the rest of the picture must stay exactly as it was, 🗇 Reconcile with input under the result lays the edit back over the input and keeps only what changed: see Reconcile with input.

A look dropped on a picture. Drag a look from the Elements panel (dusk, fog, golden autumn, a filmic finish…) onto any picture on the canvas: Brut adds an Image → Image node wired from that picture, with a Prompt holding the look's words, on the look's model when your keys reach it. Nothing runs until you press ▶, and ▶ Run prices it first. Drop a second look on that new node, run or not, to chain another change after it.

Size on Seedream: Same as the picture. On the Seedream models the Size menu of this node, of Adjust and of Compose opens with Same as the picture, and it is the default when you switch the node to Seedream: the edit comes back at the input's own pixel dimensions (on Compose, the scene's), so nothing has to be scaled or cropped when it is laid over the original in a Layers or Compare node. (The model still redraws the frame: the result can sit a few pixels off, see Keep the original outside the edit.) The model's range decides how far that holds: Pro and Flash render 0.9 to 4.6 MP, Lite 3.7 to 16.8 MP; a picture below the range comes back at the model's smallest size, one above it at its largest, with the proportions kept. Pick a size from the menu to resample on purpose. See Seedream.

Gemini gotcha: Gemini sometimes sends back no picture and no reason: that is a silent refusal (shown as OTHER or IMAGE_OTHER), usually triggered by an input image (a real person's name in facade lettering, a face, a logo), not the prompt. It happens again every time, so Brut doesn't retry it. Fix by removing or cropping the offending image.

A picture that comes from an element of your vault is named in the prompt with the element's line (a ✦ line on the face says which; untick to send the pictures bare), and the face warns when more pictures are wired than the model takes per request.

#A reference: the look to follow

The reference inlet takes the look you want: a render you already like, a photo, a material sample. The render takes its materials, colors, light and atmosphere from it, while the view, the framing and every form stay those of the picture on image. The legend under the inlets lists it as a reference. A reference alone, with nothing on image, has nothing to render: the node stops with one line and sends nothing. With nothing on reference, the node works exactly as it always did. On the Clay → Photoreal and Clay to Client Set cards, the step that writes the prompt reads the reference too: the materials, the planting, the light and the sky in the prompt are described from the reference picture, never invented from the clay, and a Materials or Vegetation knob left empty (or reading as on the reference) is answered from it. Both cards render on FLUX 3 Image: on a tower seen from two cameras with a reference photo wired, it was the one model that held each capture's view and took the reference's materials, where the others redrew the view or invented materials; the Model knob takes any other.

#Each picture its own render

Wire several pictures into image (the clay of each camera of a Scene, several viewport grabs of one project) and tick Each picture its own render: the node makes one render per picture, in wire order, instead of one render of the first picture with the others riding along. The box is greyed, saying why, until two pictures are wired; once ticked it can always be unticked.

  • One look for the set. With nothing on reference, the first render sets the look of the rest (Look of the rest: the first render): the first picture renders first, then every other picture keeps its own view and takes the first render's materials, colors, light and sky, so the building is not reinvented from one view to the next. If the first render fails, the others do not start (they would have no look to follow). Pick none, each on its own to render every picture independently. With a picture on reference, that picture sets the look of every render, the first included, and the face says so. The renders of one set come back at one size when their pictures share a shape.
  • Names in the prompt. The prompt is written once for the whole set. An @name of a reference reaches every render; an @name of one of the pictures reaches that picture's render only, and the legend says so.
  • The tiles. One tile per picture, each at its picture's own shape (the framing of every camera is what you check): the picture itself, dimmed, until its render lands, then the render (🔍 to magnify, drag it out to detach it). A render that failed turns its tile red, and its plug carries nothing until it renders; hover it for the provider's words. ↻ on a tile renders that picture again and keeps the others; on the first tile, while it sets the look, the others render again with the new look, and the price is asked first. Pin a render from 🕘 and it pins that picture alone: every row of the history names the picture it was rendered from (picture 2, say), and the pinned tile wears a 📌 and keeps that render on every run, free, until you unpin. Pin as many pictures as you like, each from any run or model. A pinned first picture is the look of the others, so they render again with it. Click the pin on the tile to unpin, or ↻ on it: it asks to unpin first. Unplug a pinned picture and its pin goes with its tile.
  • The plugs. Each tile has its own plug on its edge: wire one render into a clip, an upscale or another card, and only that render goes. The main plug carries every render, in the order of the pictures. Unplug a picture whose tile's plug is wired, and its tile stays, saying the plug carries nothing, so you can see and delete that wire. Untick the box and the tiles' plugs go, with the wires on them. A pinned picture's plug carries its pinned render, and the main plug carries the set with the pins in place.
  • No prompt, no render. A node with pictures and no prompt stops with one line and sends nothing, a reference wired or not. On a card such as Clay → Photoreal, if the step that writes the prompt fails, the renders do not go out either.
  • Never pay twice: each picture keeps its own result. Change the prompt and every picture renders again; wire a new camera and only that one renders (with the first render as its look, unchanged); a render that failed is the only one retried on the next run, the others stay. A new first render (a re-roll, a new prompt) gives the others a new look, so they render again too.
  • Cost: one image per picture, priced like any single render on that model (three cameras = three renders). ▶ Run prices the renders before anything is sent. While a node above still writes the prompt, the quote reads up to N renders (some may replay free); while a node above still has to make the pictures, the set waits for it, without a price. ▶ or ↻ on the node asks once before rendering; Keep cached only sends nothing. The node's history (🕘) holds every render.

#Adjust

One node for every correction of a picture. Paint or click where the change goes and say what goes there, or point at things with arrows and notes. Each fix is a step, each step keeps its takes, and outside each edit the picture stays the original.

Inpaint and Annotate & Correct nodes in older graphs open as Adjust nodes with one step: their mask or their marks become that step, their last result its picked take (it stays the step's result; a change you make to the step goes into its next take, with ↻). If the old node was changed after that result was made (a mask painted again, a note edited, another model picked) and saved without running, the step runs once, as the old node would have. An aspect picked on the old node is dropped: an Adjust step keeps the picture's own shape.

  1. Wire a picture into image (run upstream once so it has one).
  2. Click ✏ Start editing. The editor opens full screen on the picture, with a first step ready.
  3. Mark the change (a mask or marks, below), write what should happen, click Generate.
  4. Keep going: + on a step starts the next fix on that step's picked take.

The editor. Four fixed zones: nothing in it moves or resizes when you switch views, steps or kinds, hover a take or generate. Left: one column of tools for the selected step (the mask tools or the mark tools, in the same place, each with its key), with the keyboard button at its bottom: it shows every shortcut of the editor, and so does ?. In the middle, under a one-line bar (the active tool's settings on the left; Clear, 100 %, Fit and the picture's size and zoom on the right): the picture the step works on, with what you drew on it, or, with Picked take, the step's result drawn in exactly the same place, so the two compare in place. Show the mask (or Show the marks) lays what you drew over the result. On a mask step the mask stays editable while the result shows: see Editing the mask after a take. On a marks step nothing on the result can be drawn or changed: the tools and the marks' cards stay where they are, greyed, and Picture is one click away. The wheel zooms at the cursor and middle-drag or Space + drag (the pointer on the picture) pans, on the picture and on the result alike; 0 fits. Right: the steps, then the selected step (Picture or Picked take, Mask or Marks, its prompt, its marks, its model) and, always in view at the bottom, Keep the original outside the edit, Generate with the price, and Save. Bottom: the selected step's takes. A step is a Mask or a Marks step: pick which in its panel (once it has a take, add a new step for the other kind). Save keeps what you drew without generating; so does everything else that takes the picture away (closing the editor with Esc, picking a take, clicking another step, a new take of the step before): what you drew is kept with the picture it was drawn on. Every key the editor uses stays in the editor, whatever it shows: Delete, Ctrl+Z or Ctrl+D never touch the canvas behind it. ? shows every shortcut.

#Steps, takes and branches

  • The first row of the steps list is the picture itself: the picture wired into the node, with its thumbnail and its name (the name you gave it, else its own). It has no takes and nothing is drawn on it: click it to see the picture alone, and + on it starts a step on the original picture. The first steps hang from it, so two steps made on the picture read as a fork like any other.
  • A step is one request on one picture: the node's picture for the first step, the picked take of the step before it for the next ones.
  • Each Generate adds a take to the step and picks it. The take you picked is what the steps after it start from: a new take picks itself, and a click on another take in the bottom strip picks that one, so the next steps start from it instead. Nothing else moves a pick: editing the step, adding a step after it or generating one never switches it to another take. Hover a take to see it as the model made it, in the editor's picture, at the same place and size, the line above the picture naming that take (a take of another shape fills the frame the way it is laid back, its edges cropped, never stretched). Clicking a step that has a picked take shows that take; Picture is one click away to draw again. ↻ on a step (or on the node's title bar, for the step it shows) makes one more take of that step. The button names its price first, and a step before it that still needs a run runs with it.
  • Generating a step that has steps after it makes the node show that step: the steps after it keep their takes and nothing else is billed. Click one of them and generate it to carry the new take on (it runs again, on the new picture).
  • + on any step adds a step on its picked take. On a step that already has one after it, that is a branch: a second way to continue from the same picture. Click a step to work on it; its branch becomes the one the node shows.
  • The steps list is drawn as a tree: steps made one after the other go straight down in one column, however many there are. Only where a step (or the picture itself) has two or more steps made on it does the list indent: a line comes down and forks, and each of those steps starts a branch one level in, its own chain then straight down again. The lines of the branch the node shows are drawn in the accent color. Deeper forks indent less and less, so the list never scrolls sideways.
  • A chevron shows only where there is something to fold. Where the list forks (a step, or the picture, with two or more steps made on it), a chevron sits on the fork and folds every branch under it into one line, 3 steps folded; a click on the chevron or on that line opens them again. The first row of a branch with more rows after it carries a chevron too, which folds the rest of that branch. A branch of one row has none. Folding only tidies the list: nothing is hidden from the node, nothing runs, nothing changes, and Ctrl+Z does not count it. When the step you work on is inside a fold, wherever you picked it from, the fold opens again; while it stays folded, the folded row is outlined with a dashed line.
  • Pick another take of a step and the steps after it run again on the next run (their picture changed), and only those. Pick a take back and a step after it that was already made on that picture comes back as it was, free.
  • The node's output is the picked take of the last step of the branch shown. 🕘 on a step (or on the node, for the step it shows) lists every take that step ever made, from your history; Use this take makes one the step's picked take again, free, with the original kept outside the edit when the step keeps it. On a node opened from an older Inpaint or Annotate & Correct node, the old node's results are listed there too.
  • ✕ on a step removes it and every step made on it, after asking. Their takes stay in the history.
  • To set a branch aside without losing it, hide it: the eye on a step is open while the step is shown, and a click on it folds the step and every step made on it into one muted row (hidden · 3 steps · eye to show) with a closed eye. Nothing is deleted, its takes stay, and a click on the closed eye shows them again. A hidden step never runs and is never the one the node shows: when the node showed it, the node shows the nearest step before it. The only branch left shown can't be hidden.

#A mask: where the change goes

On a mask step, mark the area that should change, then write what goes there ("a green velvet sofa"). The tools:

  • Brush (B) and Eraser (E): the brush circle under the pointer shows the real size; [ / ] resize it. Soft feathers the edge of both, from 0 (a hard edge, the default) to 100, the way Photoshop's hardness does: the circle is where a soft brush paints at half strength, the paint fades smoothly past it, and a dashed ring shows how far (at 100, twice the circle, with only the very center fully painted). Alt+right-drag sets both, as on the Sketchpad: left and right for the size, up and down for the softness, with the numbers beside the circle. Shift+click draws a straight stroke from the end of the last one. Hold Alt as you start a stroke and the brush erases instead (the brush circle turns dashed), without switching to the eraser. On a tablet, pressure drives the size (a lighter touch, a thinner stroke) and the softness (a lighter touch, a softer edge; full pressure paints at the Soft setting), each with its own box, so either works alone. After each stroke the bar shows what Brut received, for example pen · 78 %; a tablet showing mouse · no pressure sends mouse events: turn Windows Ink on in its settings. The softness and the two boxes are remembered on this machine. On the cut (Edit the cut) a soft edge blends the take into the original; a model that takes the mask itself reads a soft edge as painted out to its middle.
  • Lasso (L): drag around an area; it fills when you let go. Hold Alt to cut an area out.
  • Polygon (P): click each corner, for straight edges (a window frame, a roof line, a wall). Close it by clicking the first corner again (a dashed ring shows where), with a double-click, or with Enter; it fills when it closes. Backspace drops the last corner, Esc cancels the polygon (it does not close the editor). Hold Alt as you close it to cut the area out. Each polygon is one Ctrl+Z.
  • Rectangle (R): drag a box; it fills when you let go, exactly the pixels inside it. Hold Alt to cut the box out, Shift for a square. Each box is one Ctrl+Z.
  • Wand (W): click an object and all of it is selected, a slat panel, a railing, a window, a tree, the sky. Each click is one object: a left click adds it to the mask, so three clicks on three objects keep all three; a right click (or Alt + click) takes the object under it out. Shift + click refines the last object: one more point that takes (Shift + right-click: one that leaves), that object worked out again with it. The bar counts the objects and how much of the picture the mask covers. Every click is one Ctrl+Z, even right after typing in the prompt or choosing in a menu. A Ctrl+Z while a click is still being worked out (Selecting…) cancels that click: nothing lands, and the next Ctrl+Z takes back the object before it.
    • A click takes: how much one click selects. Detail is the smallest piece under the click (a cushion, a handle), Part a part of the object, Whole object (the default) the largest. Your choice is for the next click: an object already taken stays exactly as it is, so switching sizes never changes what you have selected. A Shift + click refine uses the size shown when you make it. The wand only offers a size when it is confident about it: where the largest choice would spill onto the wall behind or onto the next object, Whole object gives the largest clean one instead, so two sizes can give the same selection. On an object made of many parts (a glazed door, a window of many panes), Whole object usually takes it all; if a click still takes one part, click the next part too, or Shift + click to grow that object.
    • Every object comes out clean: specks that do not touch it are left out and small holes inside it are filled; real openings (a facade's windows) stay open.
    • Grow: widens or narrows the next object, from −64 to +128 px (a shadow, a soft edge). While you drag the slider, the last object grows or shrinks on the picture so you see the result, and it takes the new value when you let go. Dragging within 1 px of zero snaps to 0; the arrow keys step one pixel at a time (so a small value such as 2 px stays reachable); a double-click on the slider or on its value puts it back to 0 px.
  • Color (F): click to fill the area of similar color; tolerance sets how close a color must be.
  • Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo, Clear empties the mask (undoable). The wheel zooms at the cursor, middle-drag or Space + drag pans, 0 fits, 100 % shows one picture pixel per screen pixel.

The wand runs on your machine, not at a provider: the first time you pick it, Brut downloads its model once (about 156 MB) and keeps it. After that a picture takes about a second to read (a fraction of a second on a graphics card) and every click answers at once. It is free, and the picture never leaves your machine for it.

The wand reads the picture first: as soon as you pick it, the line under the picture says Reading the picture… and the pointer shows that it is waiting. You do not have to wait with it: a click made meanwhile is kept, waits its turn, and lands as soon as the read is done, three clicks as three objects, in the order you made them. The line counts the clicks waiting. If you move on to another picture during the read, the clicks made on the one before are dropped, and that picture's read never blocks the new one. If the read fails, the clicks made during it are not used and the reason shows under the picture; the next click tries the read again.

Generate, Save and closing the editor wait for a click that is still being worked out, so a step never generates on the mask from before it; a click that fails stops Generate, and the reason shows under the picture. You can keep painting while a click is worked out: the object joins the mask as it is when the answer comes, and Clear drops a click still on its way, including the clicks waiting for the read.

When Brut stops answering on your machine (it was closed, or it crashed), the line under the picture says The engine is not answering within a few seconds. Your drawing stays in the editor. Save and the wand work again once Brut answers. Closing the editor still asks before it drops a drawing you have not saved.

Paint the whole object you want changed, with its shadow or reflection and a small margin: a thin stroke on one edge leaves the model to guess the rest.

Editing the mask after a take

The mask stays editable once a step has takes, in both views. With Picked take showing, any mask tool (the wand included) draws on the same mask as in Picture, and the mask shows over the take as you draw: a laid-back take and the original share the picture's frame, so the mask lands where you draw it. When the take shown is the model's own file (with Keep the original outside the edit off), the first stroke switches to Picture, then draws. Once saved, the edited mask goes into the step's next take: the picked take stays the step's result, and the steps after it keep starting from it, until you make one with ↻ New take (the button shows its price). The takes stay in the strip as the step's history. To change which part of a take shows rather than where the next take is asked, see Edit the cut.

#Marks: point and say what changes

On a marks step, point at things and say what changes, the way clients already brief on a picture. The tools (keys 1 to 7, A for the arrow): an arrow (point at the exact object), a box or a circle (hold Alt to draw from the center), a freehand lasso, a click-placed polygon (close on the first corner, with a double-click or with Enter; Backspace drops a corner, Esc cancels), a numbered point, and Select to move a mark (drag its number; on an arrow the number moves the tail, the tip re-aims it, the shaft moves the whole arrow) or a corner of a lasso or polygon; Delete removes the selected mark. Give each mark a note ("change this chair to a deep red", "remove this lamp") and, optionally:

  • a color chip: the color wheel, the RAL Classic swatch grid (216 colors with search; screen colors approximate the physical chips), or the eyedropper to take a color straight from the picture;
  • a reference picture: drop or browse a picture onto the mark's card, "replace THIS with the object shown". Formats a provider does not take (AVIF from a web page, for instance) are converted.

Each mark's card (its note, its color, its reference) sits in the panel on the right, under the prompt; select a mark on the picture and its card comes into view. The marks are the prompt: the step's own text is optional ("keep the late-afternoon light") and applies to every mark. Ctrl+Z takes the last change to the marks back. Each model gets your marks in the form it reads best, and the result comes back with no marker in it.

A marks step runs on the models that follow marks: Nano Banana 2.1 (the node's default), Nano Banana 2 and Pro, GPT Image, Seedream 5.0 Pro and Flash, and Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit. The other models stay in the step's model menu, greyed with the reason (reads no marks). On our five-mark test room Nano Banana 2.1 put every edit where its mark was drawn; the original Nano Banana applied only some edits and left markers in the result, which is why it reads no marks here. Nano Banana Pro is the strongest with many marks; GPT Image is best with a handful at a time. GPT Image 2.5 and 2.6 read your marks natively, as OpenAI built them to: in our tests each edit landed where its mark was drawn with no trace of the mark left, a mark's reference picture was followed more closely than before, and one picture less is billed per run. The marks belong to the picture, not to the model: switch the step to another model and the same marks run again, with nothing to redraw. On Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit the step runs one pass per mark, in order, each on the result of the one before: five marks are five passes at the price shown.

#Seedream on this node

On Seedream 5.0 Pro and Flash a marks step places each edit where you marked it. On a test interior with five marks, one of each kind (recolor the sofa to a RAL green, remove the coffee table, hang a painting at a point on the wall, lay a rug inside a circle, fill a shelf outlined with a lasso), then with a reference armchair placed in a box:

The five edits all five, each at its mark all five, each at its mark all five, but the painting on another wall and the rug three times the circle
Size of the result 2048 × 1143, the input's own 2048 × 1143, the input's own 2752 × 1536
Sofa color vs the RAL asked (#114232) #264933 #164335 #344d41
Cost and time $0.018, 21 s $0.045, 41 s $0.10, 16 s

One scene is not a ranking, and Nano Banana Pro was not in that run. What it does show: on Seedream an edit lands where you marked it, the result comes back at the picture's own dimensions, and a correction step on Flash costs under two cents.

  • Size: Same as the picture. On Seedream the Size menu opens with it, and it is the default: a 1376 × 768 picture comes back 1376 × 768. It holds for pictures between 0.9 and 4.6 MP; a smaller one comes back at 1K, a larger one at 2K, the model's limits. Pick 1K, 1.5K or 2K to resample: the picture's proportions are kept.
  • No 4K on a marks step. Seedream 5.0 Pro and Flash render up to 4.6 MP (2K); only Lite goes to 3K and 4K, and Lite reads no marks. For a 4K result, correct at the picture's size and finish with an Upscale Image node.
  • A mark that touches several things: name the one you mean in the note ("the lamp on the left").
  • A reference picture on a mark is one more input: on Pro each input after the first adds $0.003; on Flash inputs are free. Up to 9 reference pictures.
  • Through an OpenRouter key the step reaches the same models, at OpenRouter's sizes (1K / 2K).

#Keep the original outside the edit

A model redraws the whole picture, even with a mask or marks: outside the edit the picture comes back close to the original, not identical (fine texture redrawn, a few pixels of shift, small tone changes). This is true of every provider; OpenAI's own guide says it of its masks: "Masking with GPT Image is entirely prompt-based."

So each step, by default, keeps the original outside the edit: when a take lands, Brut lines it up with the picture the step worked on and keeps the model's result only where it changed near your mask or your marks; everywhere else the picture is the original's own pixels. It is local, free and takes a few seconds. With the switch on, nothing outside the area you asked for ever reaches the output.

Two masks, two words: the mask is where you ask for the change, before the run; the cut is which part of the take shows over the original, afterwards. Brut makes the cut for you, and you can paint it yourself: see Edit the cut.

  • What it can undo, and what it cannot. It undoes a small move of the whole frame (the model's few pixels of shift and stretch), read on the part of the picture that should not have changed, so a large change inside your mask (a lake in place of the garden) does not get in its way. It cannot undo a reframe: when the model moved the picture (another framing, a zoom, a restyle), no small move puts it back. When your mask leaves little of the picture outside it (a bare sky, a thin border), the move is read on the whole picture instead, and a take with a large change can then be refused.
  • When it is refused, the step keeps the original. Two cases, each said on the picture itself, under the bar, with the way out:
    • The model moved the picture: the original could not be laid back. The step hands the original on, unchanged. ↻ for another take, or untick Keep the original outside the edit to use this take as the model made it.
    • The model changed nothing where you asked; the change elsewhere is not used. The step hands the original on: a change the model made outside your mask or marks never reaches the output. ↻ for another take, or untick the switch to use it as made. On a mask step whose model is only shown a picture of the mask, the line also suggests GPT Image 2.5 Flare on your OpenAI key, which takes the mask itself (below). A soft change (a new tone, a warmer light) can read as nothing: Edit the cut on that take opens on your painted mask and keeps the change inside it. The take itself is kept: the strip shows what the model made, marked not used, and a hover shows it in the picture. The Activity keeps a line too. A graph saved before this behavior keeps its takes: one that was not laid back is tried again once, free.
  • Which models receive the mask itself. GPT Image on your OpenAI key and Ideogram get your mask with the request, as a mask; the other models (Nano Banana, FLUX, Seedream, and the others reached through fal.ai) are shown a picture of it, as a guide. GPT Image through fal.ai or OpenRouter takes no mask: a mask step on it stops and says so. On every model the mask steers the change rather than fencing it in (GPT Image still redraws the frame), which is why the original is laid back; a model that only sees a guide is more likely to put the change somewhere else, and that take is then refused as above.
  • The take as the model made it is always kept: hover the take in the strip to see it.
  • Turn the switch off on a step whose model is meant to change everything (a restyle, a new light): the take as the model made it flows on at once, with no new generation. Turn it back on and the laid-back picture comes back (a take made with the switch off is laid back then, free). The steps after it run again on the next run, since their picture changed.
  • When an edit removes something, its soft shadow on the wall or the floor goes with it: the original is not laid back over the place the shadow was.
  • A replaced object can come out a little larger than the area you painted (a new sofa drawn taller than the old one): a change that touches your mask is kept whole, even where it runs past it. When it runs too far, erase the spill with Edit the cut.
  • Something that did not change can stand in front of the edit: a tree trunk across a wall you recolored, a mullion, a post. When a mark points at one side, the edit on both sides of it is kept as one, and the trunk itself stays the original's. With a mask, paint across the trunk: a change your paint touches is kept, and a separate one just past it stays the original's. A change far from your mask or your marks is still left out, and so are changes scattered across the picture (foliage or gravel redrawn here and there): they are never carried along from one to the next.
  • On Ideogram 4.5 a mask is a real mask (Ideogram paints inside it and keeps the rest, and the result is the picture's own size), and Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit copies every pixel its edit did not touch: the lay-back then has little to do.

Compare before and after

With Picked take showing, press Compare in the bar above the picture, next to Edit the cut. A handle appears on the picture: on its left, the picture the step worked on; on its right, the take. Drag it across to see exactly what changed, and that nothing else did.

  • The handle stays where you put it while you zoom and pan: zoom into a detail, then slide the handle over it. A double-click on the handle puts it back in the middle.
  • Hover a take in the strip while Compare is on: that take is compared against the same picture.
  • It is the slider of the Compare node, inside the editor. It is a view only: it changes nothing in the step, costs nothing, and stays on from one step to the next until you press Compare again.
  • It is greyed in the Picture view (there is no take to compare yet) and while you edit the cut.

Edit the cut

The cut Brut makes is right most of the time. When it is not (the take's new sofa spills past the slab, a tone change you wanted was left out), you decide which part of the take shows.

  • With Picked take showing, press Edit the cut in the bar above the picture. The picture under the tools is then the live result: the original, with the take wherever the cut is. Paint where the take should show; erase where the original should come back. Every mask tool works on the cut: the brush and the eraser (Alt + brush erases; Soft feathers the edge, so the take fades into the original), the lasso, the polygon, the rectangle and the color fill, Alt taking an area out. The result updates at the end of each stroke.
  • The wand reads the take, not the original: a click picks the model's new object whole (the new sofa, and nothing around it); a right-click takes an object out.
  • Show the cut tints it, like the mask. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y step through what you did since you opened it.
  • Done (or Esc, another step, another take, Save, closing the editor) keeps the cut: the take is laid back again through it, free, on your machine, and its thumbnail in the strip carries a small ✎. The line over the picture then says the cut edited by hand.
  • Reset to the automatic cut puts back the cut Brut made, at any moment: what you painted since you opened the cut is dropped (Ctrl+Z brings it back), and a cut kept earlier goes too. You stay in the cut to paint again. It is greyed only while the cut is the automatic one and nothing is painted.
  • The cut belongs to its take: every take has its own, and a new take gets its own automatic cut. Editing the cut never touches the step's mask, so the next take is asked for the same area.
  • The steps made after it read needs a run, since the picture they start from changed (Reset brings their takes back when nothing else changed). What you painted on those steps stays where it was.
  • On a take refused because the model changed nothing where you asked, the cut opens on your painted mask: Done keeps the take's change inside it. A take refused because the model moved the picture has no cut (the button is greyed and says why), and so does a step with Keep the original outside the edit off: turning the switch off shows the take as made, turning it back on shows it through its cut again.

#Model and cost

  • Every step runs on the node's model unless you pick its own in the editor. The node's Model, Size and Quality (and Complexity, on a model that has it) are the default for every step that did not pick its own; on a workflow card, its Model knob drives them all. In the editor, on the node's model the size is the node's: Size and Quality there set the node's own (the line under them says so) and every step on the node's model takes them; a step on a model of its own has its own size and quality. When the model runs through an aggregator key, the menus offer that route's own sizes (a setting the route lacks is greyed). While a marks step runs on the node's model, the node's menu greys the models that read no marks.
  • Cost: one generation per step, at the model's price (a marks step on Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit: one per mark). Picking a take, adding or removing a step, branching, laying the original back and the wand are free.
  • Generate shows the price of what it runs before it runs (the step, and any step before it that still needs a run). ▶ on the node runs the steps that need it on the branch shown; Run on the whole graph lists them first, with the price, and asks.
  • A step needs a run when it has no take on the picture it works on: a new step, or one whose step before it now hands on another take (you picked another one there). The step list says needs a run on those steps, and Generate counts them in its price. Rarely, Generate finds an older result no longer holds as you press it: nothing runs, and the button shows the new price.
  • A step with a picked take keeps it whatever you change on it afterwards: its mask or marks, its prompt (typed or wired), its model, Size or Quality, a reference picture wired or removed, the Elements line of the node's picture, the name of a picture its prompt names with @. The change goes into the step's next take: ↻ New take in the editor (or ↻ on the node's title bar, for the step it shows) makes it, with its price on the button. ▶ on the node replays the picked takes, free.
  • A wired prompt belongs to the last step while that step has no text of its own: its line reads from the wire, and its field shows the wired text. When you move on to another step, the step keeps the wired text as its own, and its take with it; from then on it follows its own text, not the wire. A changed wired text, like a step's own text, goes into that step's next take.
  • A step's prompt and a mark's note may name the node's pictures with their @name (the legend under the inlets): @chair is the reference picture, and the name of the node's picture means the picture that step works on (the take of the step before it).
  • A mask or marks belong to the picture they were drawn on. They still fit another take of the step before at the same size when both takes keep the original outside the edit (they then share the original's framing); on any other picture (the node's own picture changed, a take of another size, or a take as the model made it, which can sit a few pixels off) the step asks you to draw them again and sends nothing. The editor shows them only where they would be sent: the others stay on the step, hidden, until you draw on that picture, which replaces them.
  • A step keeps the picture's own shape (on Gemini, the closest shape the model offers), whatever the reference pictures look like.
  • Masks, marks and takes are not saved into library items (they belong to your pictures); the steps and their prompts are.
  • If you reach Nano Banana through fal.ai or Replicate instead of your Google key, masks still steer the edit. Masks on GPT Image need your own OpenAI key.
  • Reference pictures go through the same content filters as everything else: a photo of a real person can trigger a Gemini safety refusal; the node shows it. Swap the reference.

#Reconcile with input

A generative edit redraws the whole picture. Even when the result has the same size as the input, everything in it has moved a little (a few pixels) and has been re-rendered in detail, with small tone shifts on top. On Image → Image and Compose, 🗇 Reconcile with input under the result gives you the picture you actually wanted: the original, pixel for pixel, with only the edits in it. (Adjust does this by itself at every step: see Keep the original outside the edit.)

One click spawns a Layers node, already set up and composited:

  1. The original is the base. Everything the edit did not touch stays the original's own pixels. On Compose the original is the scene; the references are not part of the stack.
  2. The result sits on top, lined up with the original, so an edit meets its surroundings without a step. The layer's row says aligned.
  3. A mask shows only what changed: the new object, the opened door, with a little room for a shadow. On these two nodes nothing points at where the change goes, so every change shows, the model's stray differences included (a hedge re-rendered, a facade tinted); paint the mask black where you want the original back.

Then the Layers editor opens on that mask, an ordinary one: paint white to show more of the edit (a longer shadow, a reflection), black to give an area back to the original. ✓ Use this stack composites again, free.

  • The node's own result is not changed. The aligned picture is a separate file that only the Layers node uses; anything else wired to the edit node keeps receiving the model's picture as it came.
  • Free and local. No provider is called; it takes a few seconds.
  • The picture comes out at the original's size. If the model returned another size (Nano Banana at 2K on a smaller input, say), the result is fitted to the original first.
  • When the two pictures can't be lined up (they have little in common), the result stays as it is and Activity says so; when no change shows, the mask starts empty and you paint it.
  • The button shows once the node has a result and an original wired in. An Image → Image node run with nothing wired (a text-to-image render) has nothing to reconcile with, so it shows none.

#Layers

Photoshop-style compositing, local and free — the node never calls a provider. A layer that carries transparency (a cutout from Ideogram 4.0 Transparent, any PNG with alpha) shows the stack below through its transparent pixels, with or without a mask.

Wire two or more images in (the bottom wire is the base), then open 🗇 Edit layers & masks. The layer tray speaks Photoshop:

  • A layer has no mask until you add one — ＋ mask on its row (click = white, reveal-all; Alt+click = black, hide-all). The brush paints the mask: white reveals, black hides — the dual swatch shows both colors (active one outlined), X swaps, D goes back to white. Alt+Backspace (or Shift+Backspace) fills the whole mask with the selected color, Ctrl+Backspace with the other one — Photoshop's foreground / background pair.
  • Brush opacity is per-stroke (1–100%): overlapping segments inside one stroke never build up. Set it with the slider or digit keys — 5 = 50%, 1 then 2 quickly = 12%, 0 = 100%. (This is the stroke's opacity; the layer's own is on its row, below.)
  • Alt + right-drag adjusts the brush — horizontal = size, vertical = softness — with the cursor pinned in place while you drag (like Photoshop); [ and ] step the size. Size, softness and opacity all have sliders too, and the live brush cursor shows the soft edge as a dashed ring. Shift+click on the image draws a straight stroke from the last stroke's end; Alt+click picks the mask's level under the cursor (white or black) as the paint color.
  • Each row: eye toggle (Alt+click = solo — hides the others; again restores), layer thumbnail, B&W mask chip — Shift+click (or Tab+click / right-click) the chip to disable the mask (red ✕, layer shows fully), Alt+click to view the mask full-frame, Ctrl+I inverts the selected mask — and the layer name (double-click to rename). Below: the layer's opacity (0–100 %: the layer shows through, so you can see what is underneath — the final composite applies it exactly the same way), feather, invert, remove mask. Drag rows to reorder; the tray under the stack duplicates (Ctrl+J), deletes (duplicates) and moves the selected layer.
  • Undo inside the editor: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) and the ↶ ↷ buttons in the tools bar step through strokes, fills, masks added or removed, layer settings and order — one step per slider drag or row drag. The canvas' own undo never fires while the editor is open. ? opens the full shortcut table.
  • Pen pressure (Wacom & co) scales the stroke width automatically.
  • Wheel zooms (past ~1.5× the preview switches to full native resolution), middle-drag pans.

Why it matters beyond convenience: generative edits keep "everything else unchanged" only approximately — the whole frame regenerates and untouched regions drift subtly. Layers clamps every unpainted region back to the source pixels. Stack the original under a generation, mask in just the part you wanted changed, and the deliverable is pixel-faithful everywhere else.

Notes:

  • A layer tagged aligned (the result layer of Reconcile with input) stays lined up with the base as long as that base is the bottom of the stack.
  • The base defines the canvas. A layer of another size auto-fits: scaled to cover and center-cropped — never distorted (the editor labels it "auto-fit"). This is the common case: a 4K generation never exactly matches its source's dimensions. Masks stay strict: an outdated mask stops the run with a clear message, never applies misaligned.
  • Masks remember which image they belong to. If an upstream node re-generates (new output), the run stops with a clear message instead of silently applying or dropping an outdated mask — reopen the editor to repaint.
  • ✓ Use this stack composites right away: the node's face shows the result the moment the editor closes — there is no ▶ on a Layers node (nothing upstream is run or re-billed; a whole-graph ▶ Run still passes through it for free). The composite is kept like any node output, and re-runs are free anyway.
  • Layers or Sketchpad? Layers reconciles finished images (masks between versions, after the generation). The Sketchpad makes an image by hand to generate from (draw, place, arrange, before the generation).
  • The other way round: Decompose result… Once the node has a result, the button under it sends that flat picture to Seedream 5.0, which splits it into a base and up to 16 layers with transparency, landed as a new Sketchpad next to the node. It is the one paid action here (the price is shown first) and needs your own BytePlus key; without it the button is greyed and says why. The same action sits on every picture's right-click menu.

#Compose

Blend a scene with one or more reference images. Wire the base image into scene, the sources you want to pull from into reference, and a prompt describing the blend. Compose is handy for dropping entourage, materials or mood from references into a render. The legend under the inlets lists the scene first, then each reference with the @name it answers to (put @armchair by the window); the names follow the Image In.

It is also the house tool for grade transfer: scene = your shot, reference = the image whose look you want, prompt = the built-in Grade match preset (in the Prompt node's Presets menu) — the palette, tones and mood carry over while the architecture stays untouched. This is the recommended finishing pass after a Multi-Shot exploration.

The same pass can borrow the reference's camera too: the Grade & camera match preset asks for its "filmic point of view" as well, and the shot is reframed to the reference's way of seeing — angle, symmetry, distance — while the building stays yours. For example, a daytime pool shot became a frontal mirror composition in one call.

When only part of the scene should take the blend, 🗇 Reconcile with input under the result stacks the scene under the result and masks the result to what changed; paint the mask to keep the rest of the scene exactly as it was (Reconcile with input).

One model on this node works without a prompt: Ideogram 4.5 Replace Background rebuilds the scene's background as the setting shown in the reference photo, keeps the people in the scene and leaves out the people in the reference, and returns the scene at its own size. Wire the picture to scene and a photo of the new setting to reference; the node says it reads no prompt. $0.22 per picture.

References that come from an element of your vault are named in the prompt (the scene first, then the references, each element with its line); the ✦ line on the face says which, untick to send them bare.

#Choosing a model

The menu always lists the current models, each with its default settings. Cheapest-to-richest on Gemini: Nano Banana 2 Lite ($0.03, text-to-image only: it can't edit) → Nano Banana 2.1 ($0.034 at 1K, up to 4K, edits and up to 14 references) → Nano Banana Pro (up to 4K). Nano Banana 2 is still on the menu, deprecated by Google with no shutdown date yet; Nano Banana 2.1 replaces it. For edits, the original Nano Banana (~$0.04) is still the cheapest and stays on the edit menus. On the BytePlus key, Seedream 5.0 undercuts all of them: Flash at $0.018 per image for exploration, Pro at $0.045 (2K $0.09) for photoreal keepers, Lite at $0.035 up to 4K. A text-to-image picture of people from any of the three (Pro, Flash or Lite, the whole picture) is accepted by Seedance as a start frame or reference; Seedream edits and crops are refused. On a Black Forest Labs key, FLUX 3 Image Klein is the cheapest of all ($0.009 at 1K, $0.014 at 2K) for many quick tries, and FLUX 3 Image the richer one that can look a real building or product up on the web before it draws. On an Ideogram key, Ideogram 4.5 is the pick for lettering and layout, Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit for a correction that must leave the rest of the picture exactly as it was, and Ideogram 4.5 Replace Background (Compose, no prompt) to put a picture's subject in another setting. See Providers for the full list and billing notes.
