Video nodes

Where stills become motion. Brut treats video like the expensive thing it is — every clip is quoted and confirmed before a cent is spent.

#Image → Video

The footer of every video node reads the clip's size, duration and frame rate (1920 × 1080 · 5.0 s · 24 fps) at the bottom left, on the output pin's row.

Animate a single image into a clip. Wire an image into the node, pick a provider and model, set duration/resolution and (where supported) audio and an end frame, and run.

Providers: **Kling** (REST and subscription), **Gemini Omni**, and **Seedance** — see [Providers](/docs/providers/index.html). Some models do text-to-video too. The node shows a **cost estimate before you run**, from each model's published prices.

Clip length follows the model: Kling offers a few fixed durations, Gemini Omni 3–10 s, Seedance 2.5 anything from 4 to 30 s (or smart, where the model picks). Where a model ties the two selects together (a resolution that renders at one length only, reference images that need a set length), the picks that cannot go together grey out in place with the reason, and a ⚠ line under the selects says what to change; where the provider's endpoint takes no audio switch, the audio box shows ticked and greyed. On models whose clip must keep the start frame's aspect ratio (Seedance 2.5), the aspect select disappears while a start frame is wired and the node says so. With nothing on the image inlet the run is text-to-video and the aspect select is back, since that run takes a ratio of your choice.

Ratio: follow the input. The aspect menu's first entry follows the start frame: the clip runs at the provider's ratio nearest to the wired image's (a 2048 × 1143 still runs at 16:9, a portrait one at 9:16), so the clip keeps the frame you composed instead of being stretched or banded to a fixed ratio. Steady camera asks the model for one smooth, stabilized move, as Film Shots does for every clip: the prompt says what stays still, and on models whose maker takes a negative prompt (Kling's API) the camera faults to avoid go there, beside your own negative prompt, never into the prompt itself. The switch cannot steady a model that trembles on every move: on Kling 3.0 (standard) a moving shot from a still shakes as much as it moves in our measurements, with the switch on or off, while a locked-off shot is clean; Kling 3.0 Omni and Seedance 2.5 held the same moves smoothly on the same frames.

Running a finished node again replays its clip, free. Want another take of the same shot? The title bar's ↻ re-roll generates a fresh one (billed again, at the same estimate); the previous clip stays in the node's 🕘 history, pinnable.

#Draft mode (Seedance 2.5)

Tick Draft take and the node renders a 480p draft, billed like any 480p clip. When the draft is the shot you want, ⬆ Render final 1080p re-renders the same shot at 1080p from the draft: BytePlus reuses the prompt, the frames, the references, the duration, the seed and the audio, nothing is re-sent, and the final is billed like any 1080p clip, after the usual confirm with its price. The final then replaces the draft on the node and on every wire (the footer line reads 1920 × 1080), while the draft stays in the node's 🕘 history. A re-roll or a changed prompt starts a new draft, and the old final stays in the ledger like every paid file. A draft can be finalized for seven days; the node's line says until when. Explore at 480p, pay 1080p only for keepers. Draft mode needs your own BytePlus key: a node running through another service such as OpenRouter shows no Draft box.

Last frame (Seedance). Seedance returns every clip's final frame as an image, at the clip's exact size, and Brut keeps it beside the clip. ⏭ Last frame → Image In under the result drops it on the canvas as an Image In: wire it into the next Image → Video's start frame and the shot continues where this one ended. When a final was rendered from a draft, the button gives the final's last frame.

The trust line (Seedance). BytePlus refuses a wired picture that looks like a real person unless one of its own models made it on your key. Brut checks where each wired picture came from, the start frame, the end frame and each reference, and says in green which ones carry that trust, with the day the 30-day window closes: made on your BytePlus key: start frame, references 1, 2. A picture counts when it is a Seedream text-to-image result or a Seedance clip's last frame. When the same set also holds pictures Seedance will refuse a face in (a Seedream edit, a picture from another model), an amber line names them, so a refusal is visible before the run. A picture with no line either way is one Brut cannot trace (an upload, a Conform cut): that is no verdict. A node fed only by other makers' pictures shows no line at all.

When a video model runs through another service (the "⇄ runs via …" chip — e.g. Kling v3 on your OpenRouter key), the node shows only the knobs that service actually accepts: settings it doesn't carry (like the aspect-ratio select or the negative prompt on OpenRouter's Kling) disappear rather than fail the run. The clip's aspect still follows the start frame you wire in. Add the native key to get the full control set back.

#Reference images

Some models accept extra reference images alongside the start frame — a character, a product, a style plate — and keep them consistent in the clip. When the selected model supports them, the node grows a references inlet and its info line says so ("✓ takes reference images (up to N)"); models that don't say that too, so you never guess. A Reference Board plugs straight in — its numbered tiles (or the whole curated pool) wire onto the inlet.

Current support: Seedance 2.5 (up to 30, addressed as @Image1… in the prompt), Gemini Omni Flash, Kling o1 / 3.0 Omni (subscription, up to 6), Seedance 2.0. Wiring references into a model that can't take them fails with a clear message — Brut never silently drops an image you wired (Kling 4.0 says so too: on this node it takes a first and a last frame only).

#Video → Video

A clip in, a new clip out. Wire a clip on the clip inlet (a Video In, an Image → Video, a Sequence, any node that makes a clip), say what you want on the prompt inlet, pick a Task, and run. Four tasks:

  • Edit the clip. Keeps the clip's camera, motion and timing and changes what your prompt names: replace an object, restyle the scene, change the season or the time of day. Wire up to 4 pictures to show what to use (the chair that should replace the sofa, the look to match). The new clip keeps the clip's framing and length.
  • Borrow the camera. A new shot that follows the clip's camera move and motion: film a move on your phone, get it back on another scene. The prompt and the wired pictures say what the shot shows; you pick its aspect ratio (it starts on the clip's own) and its length.
  • 3D preview to real shot. A clay, untextured or viewport playblast becomes a finished shot with the same geometry, camera path and timing. The look comes from the wired pictures. The surest one is a render of the same view: take a frame of the playblast with Extract frame (free), render it with Image → Image using your pictures as references, and wire that frame as the look. A portrait or a picture of the place also works, as a reference for appearance, when the prompt says what maps to what (the man in image 1 is the small seated figure of the clip) and describes the shot as the playblast frames it: the shot size, where the horizon sits, how small the figure is. A close-up wired alone, with a prompt that describes the close-up, pulls the result toward the picture: you get the portrait animated and the wide shot is gone. The prompt is optional when the look is a render of the same view.
  • Drive a character. A picture of a person or an animal performs the motion of the clip. Wire exactly one picture. Facing sets which way the character faces: as in the clip, or as in the picture (the clip then has to be 3 to 10 s).
The Task menu comes first; the Model menu then lists the models that do that task (Kling 3.0 Omni and Kling 4.0 edit, borrow a camera and render a 3D preview; Kling 3.0 and 2.6 Motion Control drive a character). On your **[Kling plan](/docs/providers/kling-subscription.html)**, or on **[Seedance 2.5](/docs/providers/seedance.html#video-to-video-your-own-storage)** with your own BytePlus key: Seedance borrows a camera, edits a clip with up to 5 pictures of what to use, and renders a 3D preview; it takes the clip and the pictures only through your own storage on BytePlus, set up once, and the node says when that key is missing.

The clip. Kling takes a clip of 3 to 10 seconds (up to 30 s on Kling 4.0, or to drive a character as the clip faces), between 720 and 2160 pixels on both sides, at 24 to 60 frames a second. On Kling 4.0 an edit is billed on the output's length alone, at a higher rate than its image-to-video (measured). The node reads the wired clip and says it under the selects (Clip: 4.0 s · 1076 × 1926 · 24 fps). A clip outside the size or frame-rate range is resized or resampled on your machine before it is sent, and the line says so (→ sent at 960 × 720); a clip outside the length range is never cut for you: the node says to trim it first (the Sequence's in and out points do that, free).

What blocks a run is said before it. No clip, no prompt where the task needs one, too many pictures, no character for Drive a character, a clip too long or too short: a ⚠ line under the selects names it, and the run stops there without calling anyone.

Cost. Every run asks first, like every video node, with the clip's length and your plan balance in the question. Edit the clip and 3D preview to real shot at 720p are measured: about 9 credits a second (36 credits for a 4-second clip, about $0.39 at the Pro rate). The other tasks and resolutions show no estimate until measured; compare your plan balance before and after. A finished node replays its clip for free; ↻ re-roll makes a new take, and the old one stays in 🕘 history.

Time. A video-to-video render can take a while on Kling's side, well past the length of the clip, on a busy day. Brut waits up to an hour (the job survives an engine restart, like every video job). A run that still times out keeps its task id on the node and in the ledger, and the clip, once finished, can be fetched from your kling.ai gallery: the timeout itself bills nothing more.

#Seedance assets

Seedance refuses a picture with a face in it, generated or not, unless BytePlus made the picture or the picture is registered in your BytePlus asset library. This node registers. Wire pictures of your people into it (a Character Lab's outputs, an Image → Image, an Image In), name the Set (the node proposes your project's name) and click Register: each picture is copied into your asset library through your own storage, in about a second, free, and Seedance accepts the people in it from then on, as a start or end frame, a reference or a picture beside a clip, in every graph and every project.

  • The pictures pass through. What flows out is what flows in, in wire order, registered or not, then the assets you picked from your library. Put the node anywhere between the pictures and the video node; nothing downstream changes and nothing re-runs. There is no ▶: Register is a click, and a whole-graph run passes the pictures on.
  • Pick from your library… opens every asset on your BytePlus account in one grid, with pictures, each card naming its set and the day it was registered. Tick the ones you want and click Use: their pictures are downloaded into this project's inputs and flow out of the node, already accepted by Seedance; a picked tile carries a ✕ on the picture that takes it off the node only. Tick and Delete from library removes assets from BytePlus after a confirmation.
  • The tiles say the state. Each picture shows full width with either its asset id (✓ registered) or not registered, with a field to paste an id you registered by hand in the BytePlus console. The Register button counts what is left to register; a picture BytePlus refuses as an asset is named on the node. The Set field is the name of the group new registrations are filed under, never a place for an asset id; the long explanation of the node sits on hover of its first line.
  • It remembers by content. A registration follows the picture's pixels, not its path: the same file under another name, or in another project, is the same asset. A re-export or a crop is another picture and needs its own registration.
  • The quota. The free BytePlus plan holds 50 assets; the line under the buttons says how many your account holds. ⚙ Settings → BytePlus storage shows the same library and deletes an asset on request. Nothing is registered or removed on its own.
  • The key. It needs the same BytePlus storage key as Video → Video, with the Ark rights beside the storage ones. The node says when the key is missing; the paste field works without it.
  • On Image → Video, a registered picture reads green in the trust line (registered as a BytePlus asset), and the ledger row of a clip lists the asset ids it went out on. The details and the rules on whose faces may be registered are on the Seedance page.
## Film Shots — the cinema batch executor

Film Shots takes a shot plan (from the Director / Storyboard) and shoots the whole sequence.

Prompts written for the model you picked. Before a clip is shot, Film Shots adapts the Director's prompt to the video model you picked, automatically (one cheap text call per clip, cents, in the ledger). There is nothing to set: the table marks adapted rows with ✦ (hover one to read the Director's text), and the confirmation says how many were adapted. A clip whose adaptation fails is flagged on its row and in the confirmation, and goes out with the Director's text.

Its defining feature is the cost guard:

  • It prices every clip — model, duration, resolution, estimated cost — and shows an itemized confirmation before any spend. Cancel a line and the total updates.
  • The table follows the plan, before and after a run. Add a shot on the Storyboard, re-plan the Director with more or fewer shots, change a clip's prompt: the rows change at once. A clip that already rendered keeps its result on its row as long as its request is the one that ran; a changed clip is queued again, a dropped one leaves the table, a new one appears priced.
  • Tick boxes choose what gets sent. Every row has one, all ticked by default; untick a clip and it stays in the table, greyed, not sent, not priced, not in the output pool — generate three, judge them, then tick the rest. The Σ row's box ticks or unticks everything, and its N of M clips line always says what Generate would actually send. Ticks are saved with the graph (never in library items).
  • Clips already made (unchanged) and pinned clips cost $0 and are never re-quoted.
  • A ruled resolution snaps the plan's durations. On a model whose resolution renders at one length only, every clip in the table snaps to it, the line under the Resolution select says so, and the confirm lists the snapped rows. A row that still breaks a rule is blocked with the fix, like an invented shot without its keyframe.
  • It shoots several clips at a time, with the same per-clip controls as Multi-Shot: free replay / ↻ re-roll / ✓✗ verdicts / 📌 pins.
  • On Kling (subscription) without a subscription, kling.ai runs one clip at a time and watermarks it: clips refused for that reason wait and run one by one after the pool, the row saying so on hover, the Activity panel saying why once.
  • Every clip asks for a steady camera, and a clip gets sound only when you switch audio on and the model makes sound.
  • An invented shot whose keyframe hasn't been generated on the Storyboard is a per-clip error — that clip is blocked (and counted in the confirm), never silently downgraded to text-to-video. The rest of the batch rolls normally. A shot added on the Storyboard with no clip prompt yet is listed and blocked the same way, the row naming the fix, instead of vanishing from the table.
  • Draft mode on Seedance 2.5: tick Draft takes and every clip rolls at 480p, billed like 480p. Once the drafts are in, each row grows a final 1080p tick; ⬆ Render N finals re-renders exactly those shots at 1080p from their drafts, after its own itemized confirm. A finalized row's output becomes the final, on the Clip Board and in the Sequence downstream, while the draft stays in 🕘. Seven days to finalize a draft; your own BytePlus key only.
Nothing is generated until you confirm the total. Other branches keep running while it waits.

Like Image → Video, a model reached through another service (the "⇄ runs via …" chip) shows only the knobs that service accepts — the quote table and the clips both use exactly what will be sent, and the film's aspect rides each clip's start frame.

#Clip Board

The review board for Film Shots — video tiles, ✓ / ✗ / ↻ per clip, and a plug per tile to route one clip onward. The video equivalent of the Shot Board.

Tiles show a still poster of each clip (in the clip's own aspect ratio) and stay feather-light — press ▶ on a tile to play it; one clip plays at a time, and 🔍 opens it full-screen — ←/→ screens the whole board clip by clip. The /row select goes up to 15, so a whole reel can sit in one row.

#Extract frame

Takes one still out of each clip wired into it, at the clip's own size, on your machine. Free.

  • Last frame (the default) is the continuity move: wire it into the next shot's Image → Video as the start frame, and the next clip begins exactly where this one ended. Kling and Gemini Omni give back no last frame of their own; this works with every video model.
  • First frame, or at a time: a slider runs through the clip one frame at a time, with the time and the frame number under it, and you can type the time in seconds. While you drag, the clip itself shows that moment; the frame is taken when you let go.
  • Several clips wired in give one frame each, in the order of the wires. A time past the end of a shorter clip takes that clip's last frame, and the node says how many clips it happened to.
  • The frames flow out as pictures: into an Upscale Image for a delivery still, an Image → Image, a Storyboard card's socket, a Preview. Right-click a frame for Reveal and Export, like any picture.
  • There is no ▶: the node takes its frames as soon as the clips, the choice or the time change, and a frame it already took comes back at once.
## Sequence — local assembly, free

Wire your curated clips into a Sequence node and it assembles them into one mp4 on your machine — cuts or chained crossfades, scaled and padded to a common size. It's free, and an unchanged assembly replays at once.

- Nothing to install: the video tool ships inside Brut. - The film **follows the first clip's orientation** — a pool of vertical 9:16 clips assembles into a vertical reel, not a pillarboxed landscape frame. The Quality select sets the short side (1080p vertical = 1080 wide). - **Clip audio is kept.** Clips with native sound (Gemini Omni, Seedance, Kling with audio on) carry it into the film, in sync even beside silent clips; crossfades fade the audio too. A pool with no sound anywhere produces a clean video-only file. - **Trim each clip.** Every clip in the list has an **in** and an **out** point in seconds, with its length beside them: cut the first second of a clip that starts on a shaky frame, or the last one where a clip morphs at its end. ↺ puts the clip back whole. The film's length under the list follows the trims and the crossfades. A trimmed clip keeps at least 0.1 s, and more than the fade when you crossfade; the row says so before you run. A trim stays with its clip wherever you move it in the list, and the sound is cut with the picture. - **Every wired clip is in the list**, wire after wire, in the order the film plays them: wire two clip nodes into the Sequence and both show, each with its own in and out. ↑ and ↓ move a clip anywhere in the film, across wires. ## The full flow

The video nodes are the back half of the Cinema Sessions: Storyboard → Film Shots → Clip Board → Sequence → Preview. Read that walkthrough to see them working together.
