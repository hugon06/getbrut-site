Seedream

Seedream (BytePlus ModelArk) is ByteDance's image family. Brut carries the Seedream 5.0 trio since 2026-10-02: Pro, Flash and Lite, for text-to-image and image-to-image, on the same BytePlus key as Seedance. It is the cheapest photoreal tier in Brut, and the one BytePlus trusts for people that Seedance will animate.

#Get a key

Nothing new to paste. Seedream rides your Seedance key automatically: ⚙ Settings → Seedream reads uses your Seedance (BytePlus) key. Set a key there only to split Seedream's billing from Seedance's.

Activate the models first. Like every ModelArk model, each one is switched on once per account: open the console's Model activation page (console.byteplus.com/ark) and activate Seedream 5.0 Pro, Seedream 5.0 Flash and Seedream 5.0 Lite. No resource pack is needed: activation is a one-time switch and the images bill pay-as-you-go on the account's credits or card (see Seedance, pricing). Until then a call fails with "has not activated the model", shown verbatim on the node. Lite comes with a free quota of 50 images.

Pro is the quality tier (BytePlus's pick for "commercial visual production": precise editing, lifelike scenes). Flash is the same family at a third of the price, for exploration and high-volume passes. Lite is the older line with the biggest canvases and 50 free images. Speed, measured on 2026-10-02: Flash about 15 s for a 1K image, Pro about 30 s, Lite about 75 s at 2K. 1.5K bills like 1K and BytePlus calls it the better quality pick, so Pro and Flash default to it; Lite defaults to 2K.

All three appear wherever an image model is picked: Text → Image, Image → Image, Inpaint, Compose, Multi-Shot, the Storyboard's keyframes, the Sketchpad's generation layers, the workflow cards' Model knobs. On Annotate & Correct, Pro and Flash are on the menu with their own way of reading the marks; Lite is not.

#What Brut sends

  • An explicit width × height, always. With a bare resolution preset, BytePlus lets the model decide the final size from the prompt (the same trap as OpenAI's size: auto). Brut computes the pixels itself: the standard aspects use BytePlus's own mapping table (a 16:9 image at 1K is 1424 × 800, at 2K 2816 × 1584; a 4K Lite 9:16 is 3040 × 5504), any other aspect gets the exact ratio at the tier's pixel budget. On an edit, aspect auto keeps the input's own proportions, and when the input already sits in the size you picked (a 2048 × 1143 render at 1.5K, say) it comes back at exactly its own dimensions, pixel for pixel: Seedream accepts any width × height inside its bounds (we ran a 1423 × 801 edit and got 1423 × 801 back). Pick a bigger or smaller size and the output moves to that size at the input's aspect: on Pro and Flash to the pixel (a 1376 × 768 picture at 1.5K is asked as 2064 × 1152, exactly one and a half times), on Lite to the nearest multiple of 16. A picture that sits in a smaller size than the one picked is therefore resampled.
  • Edits: Same as the picture. On Image → Image, Inpaint, Compose and Annotate & Correct, the Size menu opens with Same as the picture on all three Seedream models, and it is what the node takes when you switch it to Seedream. Brut measures the input (the scene, on Compose) and asks for exactly its pixel dimensions, so the result lays over the original in a Layers or Compare node with nothing to scale. Each model's range sets the limit: Pro and Flash 0.9 to 4.6 MP, Lite 3.7 to 16.8 MP; below it the picture comes back at the model's smallest size, above it at its largest, the proportions kept. A node that already carries a picked size keeps it, and a stage hidden inside a workflow card keeps the size its card set: both have "Same as the picture" one pick away.
  • PNG, lossless downstream (Layers, PSD export, Seedance frames), and watermark off: BytePlus stamps an "AI-generated" mark bottom right by default, Brut always turns it off.
  • Your input files' exact bytes. Inputs travel as they are, never re-encoded or downscaled (that is what keeps BytePlus's trust signal on a Seedream original, below). The limits are checked before the call and fail loudly with the fix: 30 MB per image, 196 px² to 36 MP, both sides above 14 px, aspect between 1:16 and 16:1.
  • Inpaint masks are guidance. Seedream has no native mask input, so the Inpaint node sends the Gemini-style guide image (the painted region in white) plus the confine-the-edit preamble. Strong steering, never a pixel lock; stack the result over the original in Layers when the rest must stay exact.
  • Output URLs live 24 hours on BytePlus's side; Brut downloads every image at once.

#People for Seedance: the trusted path

ModelArk's moderation refuses Seedance start frames and references that look like they contain a real person, and it cannot tell a generated face from a photograph (see the Seedance gotchas). The exception BytePlus documents: since 2026-04-16, face-containing images generated by Seedream on your own account are trusted as Seedance 2.x input for 30 days (the doc names Lite text-to-image). Seedance's own clips and their frames are trusted the same way.

We measured it with real requests and a clear face in frame, on Seedance 2.5, 2.0 Fast and 2.0 Mini, as a start frame and as a reference:

The picture Seedance
Seedream text-to-image, on Lite, Pro or Flash, the whole picture accepted
One text-to-image character sheet (several views and expressions in one picture) accepted
A frame of a Seedance clip (its last frame, or one taken from the middle) accepted
Any Seedream edit (Image → Image), on Lite, Pro or Flash, even of a text-to-image picture refused
A crop of an accepted picture refused
The same portrait made by another maker refused

A resize and a re-export (PNG, JPEG) keep the trust. A crop loses it, and so does anything that redraws the picture. An edit only gets through when no face is visible in it (people seen from behind, eyes hidden), which is the detector not firing, not trust: do not build on it.

What this means in practice:

  • One populated picture: write it as a Text → Image prompt on any Seedream model and wire the result, uncropped, into Image → Video.
  • A character: the picture Seedance accepts is the one made from text. With the Character Lab, that is the design output when nothing is wired into character; its expression and turnaround sheets are edits of the design, so Seedance refuses them on every model. To give Seedance several views, write one text-to-image picture that is the whole sheet (full-body views in one row, expression close-ups in another) and wire that single picture as the reference.
  • Continuing a shot: the last frame of a Seedance clip is accepted as the next clip's start frame.
  • People added to an existing render (an edit): refused when a face shows. Keep faces out of view, or put the people in with the video prompt.

When Seedance refuses a picture, the node's message says which wired picture it was (the start frame, reference 2) and repeats this rule. And before any run, the Image → Video node reads the ledger and shows which wired pictures carry the trust: see the trust line.

#Decompose into layers

Seedream 5.0 Pro and Flash can split one flat picture into a base (the scene with the elements lifted out and the gaps filled in) plus up to 16 layers with transparency, each with a name, a description and its place in the picture. In Brut: right-click any picture → Decompose into layers… (outputs, imports, board tiles, the magnifier), or Decompose result… under a Layers node's result.

  1. A small form asks for the model (Flash by default: the cheapest, and the one measured), the size and, if you want, what to separate ("the sky, the building, the trees in front, the people"). Leave that empty and the model picks the main elements itself.
  2. The price is shown before anything is spent. BytePlus bills every picture it returns, the base included: $0.018 each on Flash; on Pro $0.0225 each up to 2.61 MP and $0.045 above, each picture on its own pixels. How many layers come back is the model's call, so the dialog states the ceiling (16 layers and the base, 17 pictures: $0.31 on Flash) and the ledger row is priced from what actually came back. Measured on Flash: a 2752 × 1536 render split into a base and three layers (the sky, the building, the trees) in just under a minute for $0.072.
  3. The result lands as a new Sketchpad beside the picture: the base at the bottom, every layer above it in stacking order, scaled into its place and named by the model, and the original on top, hidden, one click on its eye away for a comparison. The node shows the recomposed picture at once. From there: move, scale, hide or mask a layer, paint between them, add a generation layer, or Export PSD and carry on in Photoshop.

The details:

  • Size. Same as the picture (the default) brings the base back at the picture's own dimensions when it sits between 0.9 and 4.6 MP, so the stack lines up with the original pixel for pixel; a smaller picture comes back at 1K, a larger one at 2K. With this setting each layer also comes back at exactly its size in the picture, so nothing is rescaled. Or pick 1K, 1.5K or 2K yourself: the layers then come back near that resolution, usually larger than their place in the picture (detail to spare if you scale one up), and Brut scales them into place.
  • One picture in, from 512 × 512 px to 36 MP, up to 30 MB. PNG and JPEG travel as they are; any other format (WEBP, TIFF) is converted to PNG on the way, losslessly.
  • The model lifts each element out whole. Trees that stood behind a hedge can come back with their full trunks, which then show in front of the base. The words you give it steer what comes back; and in the Sketchpad a layer mask hides the part that should stay behind (select the layer, add a mask, paint black over the trunks).
  • All or nothing. If one layer fails on BytePlus's side the whole call fails, and the message is shown as it came. Nothing lands on the canvas.
  • Your own BytePlus key. The ability is not sold through OpenRouter: on a seat without the key the menu item stays in place, greyed, and says why.
  • Every file is kept in the project's outputs, named after the run (…_sketch-12-layers_seedream_SR5F_…, then -2, -3 in stacking order). Find in ledger on the Sketchpad's preview, or on any of the files, shows the run with its cost.

#Annotate & Correct: marks as coordinates

Seedream 5.0 Pro and Flash read positions written into the prompt: a point or a rectangle, as numbers between 0 and 999 across the picture. The Annotate & Correct node uses exactly that. Draw your marks as usual, pick Seedream on the node, run: Brut sends the clean picture alone and writes each mark's place into its numbered instruction. No copy with red markers goes out, so there is nothing for the model to mistake for part of the picture.

  • An arrow or a point marks a spot (for the arrow, its tip). A box, circle, lasso or polygon marks its bounding box.
  • A mark's reference image is sent after the picture and named in that mark's instruction ("use the subject shown in Image 2"). A color chip goes by name and hex value.
  • Size: Same as the picture is the default on this node for Seedream: the result comes back at the picture's own dimensions (between 0.9 and 4.6 MP; a smaller picture comes back at 1K, a larger one at 2K), so the result and the original stack at the same size in a Layers node. Pick 1K, 1.5K or 2K to resample instead.
  • The same size is not the same pixels: the model redraws the frame, and a result can sit a few pixels off its original (3 to 6 px on a 2752 × 1536 scene we measured). 🗇 Reconcile with input lays the result back over the original and keeps only the edits: everything else is the original's own pixels (how it works).
  • When a box holds several things, name the one you mean in the note: the model gets the rectangle, not your outline.

Measured on a 2048 × 1143 interior with five marks, one of each kind: both models applied all five at their marks and returned 2048 × 1143; Flash in 21 s for $0.018, Pro in 41 s for $0.045 (Pro matched the asked RAL green more closely). A second run placed an armchair from a reference image inside a box, turned the way the note asked, on both. The same marks through the drawn-copy route on Nano Banana 2 were all applied too, with two of them placed loosely. The table is on the Annotate & Correct page.

#What it costs

  • The node shows the per-image price before the run (the output price: $0.045 / $0.018 / $0.035, Pro 2K $0.09). The ledger row is priced from what came back: the actual output pixels decide the tier (1K and 1.5K bill the same; above 2.61 MP is the 2K rate), and Pro's input-image surcharge is added from the count BytePlus reports ($0.003 per input after the first). Pinned on the first live runs (2026-10-02): every requested size came back exactly, and the usage block carries the token count (width × height ÷ 256) and the input count the ledger math uses.
  • A moderation refusal is not billed and the node says so.
  • A decomposition bills per picture returned (the base and each layer), at its own rate: $0.018 on Flash, $0.0225 or $0.045 on Pro. The price dialog states the ceiling, the ledger the real amount.
  • No BytePlus key? All three also run through an OpenRouter key, marked ⇄ runs via OpenRouter on the node, with OpenRouter's size menu (1K / 2K for Pro and Flash, 2K / 4K for Lite; no 1.5K or 3K there) at OpenRouter's listed pass-through prices. The ledger records the exact billed amount of every routed job.

#Gotchas

  • Lite's smallest output is 2K (its pixel floor is 3.7 MP); Pro and Flash render between 0.9 and 4.6 MP. The size menus follow each model.
  • Why Pro and Flash stop at 2K. It is BytePlus's limit, not Brut's: those two models render at most 4.62 MP, which is a 2K picture (2816 × 1584 at 16:9). 3K and 4K exist on Lite only. For a 4K Seedream picture, use Lite on Text → Image, Image → Image, Inpaint or Compose; for a 4K result from a Pro or Flash edit, add an Upscale Image node after it.
  • BytePlus recommends prompts under about 600 English words: longer prompts lose details rather than gain them.
  • Sequential sets (Lite, up to 15 related images in one call) and transparent-background edits exist on the API and are planned for Brut. Layer decomposition and coordinate-addressed editing are in: see Decompose into layers and Annotate & Correct.
  • You can also type the coordinate tags yourself in any Image → Image prompt on Pro or Flash (<point>520 460</point>, <bbox>120 180 640 760</bbox>, 0 to 999 from the top-left corner, the picture named "Image 1"): Brut passes the prompt as written. The Annotate node exists so you never have to.
